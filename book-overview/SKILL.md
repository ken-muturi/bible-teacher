# Book Overview — Research Skill

Produces a structured research brief for any of the 66 books of the Bible.
Use this BEFORE writing a video outline. It is research, not content.

**Prerequisite:** teacher-foundation must be set up.

---

## Input

Provide:
- **Book name** (required)
- **Series context** (optional — e.g., "this is video 3 in my Torah series")
- **Specific questions** (optional — "I'm confused about the authorship debate" or "focus on the exile connection")

---

## Output: Seven Research Sections

### 1. Book Identity (1 paragraph)
- Genre and literary type
- Author (confirmed, disputed, or anonymous — say which)
- Date of writing and original audience
- Where it sits in the biblical canon and why that placement matters

### 2. The Big Story / Argument (2-3 paragraphs)
- For narrative books: the plot arc in plain terms
- For epistles/wisdom: the central argument or movement of thought
- The problem the book is addressing and how it resolves

### 3. Historical & Cultural Background (2-3 paragraphs)
- Political situation, religious assumptions, cultural practices
- What a first-century (or first-millennium BC) reader would have understood that we miss
- One or two specific details that change how you read the book

### 4. Key Word Study (3-5 words)
For each word:
- Transliterated Hebrew or Greek term
- Literal meaning and range of meaning
- How major translations render it differently (ESV, NIV, NLT)
- Why it matters for understanding the book

### 5. Interpretive Pressure Points (3-5 questions)
Questions the text raises that honest teachers have to engage:
- Authorship or dating debates (if significant)
- Passages commonly misread or taken out of context
- Theological tensions the book holds without resolving
- Places where responsible interpreters disagree

### 6. Cross-Bible Connections (4-6 links)
Categorized as:
- **Backward links** — OT texts this book quotes, echoes, or fulfills
- **Forward links** — how this book sets up later Scripture
- **Thematic parallels** — other books dealing with the same theme differently

### 7. Application Bridge (2-3 paragraphs)
- What changed for the original audience as a result of this text?
- What is the modern equivalent of that situation?
- One concrete question your audience can sit with after watching

---

## Critical Constraints

- Never fabricate commentary positions or scholar quotes
- If authorship is disputed, say so and name both positions
- Do not produce a video outline — this is research only
- Flag any section where AI confidence is low and suggest verification sources
- Use the teacher's preferred translation throughout; show original language only when it genuinely matters

---

Run **book-overview-infographic** to generate a visual teaching panel (HTML) from this brief.
