# Video Outline — Script Structure Skill

Turns a completed book-overview brief into a structured video outline.
This produces a talking-points outline, not a word-for-word script.

**Prerequisite:** teacher-foundation + a completed book-overview brief for this book.

---

## Input

Provide:
- **Book name**
- **Target length** (default: 18-22 minutes)
- **Anything to emphasize** (optional — "spend more time on the historical background" or "audience asked about the authorship question")
- **Anything to skip** (optional — "assume they've already seen my Genesis video")

---

## Output Structure

### Hook (60-90 seconds)
One of three approaches — pick the strongest for this book:
- **The surprising fact**: something historically true that most people don't know
- **The modern parallel**: a contemporary situation that mirrors the book's original problem
- **The honest question**: something a curious non-religious person would genuinely want answered

Do not open with "Today we're going to look at..." or "The book of X is..."

---

### Part 1 — What Is This Book? (3-4 minutes)
Cover in plain language:
- Who wrote it, when, and for whom (acknowledge uncertainty if it exists)
- What kind of writing it is (genre shapes everything)
- One sentence on where it sits in the larger biblical story

---

### Part 2 — The Big Picture (5-6 minutes)
The narrative arc or central argument, section by section.
Format: brief heading + 2-3 talking points per section.
Include one key passage to read aloud (30-60 seconds).

---

### Part 3 — What Did It Mean Then? (4-5 minutes)
The historical/cultural moment the book was written into.
- One or two specific background details that reframe the book
- What the original audience would have heard that we miss
- The interpretive question most people get wrong about this book

---

### Part 4 — So What? (4-5 minutes)
The application bridge. Structure it as:
- **Then:** What this text asked of its original audience
- **Now:** The modern equivalent situation or tension
- **Question:** One thing to sit with — not an answer, an honest question

Do not moralize. Do not tell the audience what to believe. Give them something to think about.

---

### Close (60-90 seconds)
- One-sentence summary of the book's core contribution to the Bible's larger story
- What's next in the series (tease the next book or a thematic connection)
- Simple call to action (subscribe, join discussion, leave a question)

---

## Format Rules

- Use talking-point bullets, not full sentences (except for the key passage read-aloud)
- Mark any place where the teacher should pause for emphasis: **[PAUSE]**
- Mark places where a visual would help: **[VISUAL: suggest what]**
- Total bullet points: 35-50 (enough to cover 18-22 minutes at a natural pace)
- Flag any claim that needs fact-checking before filming: **[VERIFY: what to check]**

---

## What This Skill Will Not Produce

- A word-for-word script
- Theological conclusions the teacher hasn't reached themselves
- Fabricated quotes attributed to scholars or commentators
- An outline that works without the teacher's own knowledge and preparation
