Faith Rooted in Scripture: Rebuilding on the Word
Amos 9:11–13
Year theme — strengthening families & ministries
I. Connecting
Share what happened in your lives during the last week. Use an ice-breaker or other approach to get to know each other better.
II. Seeking God
(If your group is larger than 8 persons, break into groups of 3–4 for this.) Discuss any personal roadblocks, challenges, or fears that threaten your ability to walk by faith. Pray about things that will really make a difference in your life today.
III. Lesson Time
"In that day I will raise up the fallen shelter of David. I will repair its broken walls and restore its ruins, and will rebuild it as it used to be… New wine will drip from the mountains and flow from all the hills." Amos 9:11, 13 (NIV)
Observation
1.After chapters of judgment, Amos 9:11 suddenly turns to hope. What three things does God say he will do to "the fallen shelter of David" — and notice who is doing the acting. Whose effort is the rebuilding resting on?
2.In Acts 15:13–18, James quotes Amos 9:11–12 to explain what God is doing in the early church. What does he say the rebuilt "tent of David" turns out to be — and how does that root the church's very identity in a written promise?
3.Amos 9:13 pictures a harvest so overflowing the plowman catches up to the reaper, and speaks of "the one who sows the seed." Jesus later says plainly, "the seed is the word of God" (Luke 8:11). How does that connect this abundant harvest to God's word being planted?
4.Count how often "declares the LORD" / "says the LORD" anchors these three verses. What is that repeated phrase telling us about where the certainty of this restoration comes from — and what it must be built on?
Application
1.God promises to rebuild what has fallen. Is there a family relationship or ministry in your life that feels "fallen" or in ruins? On what have you been trying to rebuild it — your own effort, other people, circumstances — and what would it mean to rebuild it on God's word instead?
2.The restoration in Amos is emphatically God's doing — "I will raise up… I will rebuild." Where are you wearing yourself out trying to rebuild in your own strength something that only God's promise can actually raise?
3.Our year theme is strengthening families and ministries. Name one concrete way you could sink the roots of your family or ministry deeper into Scripture this year, so that when drought comes it still stands and bears fruit (cf. Amos 9:15 — "planted… never again to be uprooted").
4.True or False: A family or ministry can look busy and active on the surface while its roots in God's word have quietly dried up.
Prayer
LORD, you are the God who raises up what has fallen and rebuilds the ruins.
We cannot rebuild our families and ministries in our own strength — only you can.
Root us deep in your word, past the surface, down into the heart.
Plant us so we are never again uprooted, and let the harvest overflow, for your glory.
Quote
"The vigour of our spiritual life will be in exact proportion to the place held by the Bible in our life and thoughts."
— George Müller
Memory Verse
Amos 9:11 — "In that day I will raise up the fallen shelter of David. I will repair its broken walls and restore its ruins, and will rebuild it as it used to be."
IV. Accountability
Break into small groups of 3–4 persons. Recite your memory verses from the last few weeks. Share what you have done/learned in your daily devotion times. Share how you have sought opportunities to build relationships with unbelievers on your evangelism list, and the outcome of this.
V. Building Community
Do an exercise or have a discussion that builds stronger and deeper relationships. Make announcements and plans that affect the entire group.
VI. Closing
Close in prayer. Enjoy refreshments and fellowship.
