Justice: Let It Roll Like Waters
The Book of Amos
I. Connecting
Share what happened in your lives during the last week. Use an ice-breaker or other approach to get to know each other better.
II. Seeking God
(If your group is larger than 8 persons, break into groups of 3–4 for this.) Discuss any personal roadblocks, challenges, or fears that threaten your ability to walk by faith. Pray about things that will really make a difference in your life today.
III. Lesson Time
Observation
1.Amos opens with a string of oracles against Israel's neighbours — Damascus, Gaza, Tyre, Edom, Ammon, Moab — before turning the same formula on Judah and finally on Israel itself. What is the effect of saving Israel for last, and how do you think the first listeners felt as the speech reached them?
2.When the priest tells him to go home, Amos answers, "I was no prophet, nor a prophet's son, but I was a herdsman and a dresser of sycamore figs." What does he seem to be saying about where his authority comes from?
3.In 4:1 Amos addresses the "cows of Bashan" — the comfortable women of Samaria. What picture does that image paint, and who is it aimed at?
4.In 5:21–24 God says, "I hate, I despise your feasts," and refuses their songs and offerings. What reason does the text give for rejecting worship that God himself had commanded?
Application
1.Amos speaks into a boom time — the reign of Jeroboam II, with expanding borders and thriving trade. How do we tell the difference between real flourishing and a prosperity that is quietly built on someone else's loss?
2.In chapter 7 God holds up a plumb line — a builder's tool that shows whether a wall is truly straight. If justice is that plumb line, what would you hold it against in your own church, workplace, or city, and what might turn out to be leaning?
3.Amos insists that singing, giving, and gathering mean nothing to God while the poor are being crushed. Where do you see the same gap today between the worship people offer and the way they treat the vulnerable?
4.True or False: A person can worship God faithfully on Sunday and still be part of an injustice the rest of the week.
Prayer
Lord, teach us to want justice as much as we want comfort.
Search our worship, and strip away anything that is only for show.
Open our eyes to the people our systems overlook.
Let justice roll through us like water, and make our lives an ever-flowing stream.
Quote
"Few are guilty, but all are responsible."
— Abraham Joshua Heschel, The Prophets
Memory Verse
Amos 5:24 — "But let justice roll down like waters, and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream."
IV. Accountability
Break into small groups of 3–4 persons. Recite your four memory verses from the last four weeks. Share what you have done/learned in your daily devotion times. Share how you have sought opportunities to build relationships with unbelievers on your evangelism list, and the outcome of this.
V. Building Community
Do an exercise or have a discussion that builds stronger and deeper relationships. Make announcements and plans that affect the entire group.
VI. Closing
Close in prayer. Enjoy refreshments and fellowship.
