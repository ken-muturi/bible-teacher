The Glory That Leaves — and Comes Back
The Book of Ezekiel
I. Connecting
Share what happened in your lives during the last week. Use an ice-breaker or other approach to get to know each other better.
II. Seeking God
(If your group is larger than 8 persons, break into groups of 3–4 for this.) Discuss any personal roadblocks, challenges, or fears that threaten your ability to walk by faith. Pray about things that will really make a difference in your life today.
III. Lesson Time
Observation
1.In chapters 8–11 the glory of God rises from the holy place, pauses at the threshold, moves to the east gate, and finally leaves the city. Why do you think the departure is shown in stages rather than all at once, and what might that pacing reveal about God?
2.In the valley of dry bones (ch.37) the bones first come together and are covered with flesh, but stay dead until the breath (ruach) enters them. Why is the vision staged in two steps — assembly first, then breath — and what does that say about what real restoration requires?
3.God promises to remove the "heart of stone" and give a "heart of flesh," putting his own Spirit within (36:26). Look closely at the wording: who does the acting in that promise, and what does the people's part appear to be?
4.The phrase "you will know that I am the LORD" recurs around seventy times, after both judgment and rescue. What does it tell us that God gives the same goal for wrecking a city and for raising the dead?
Application
1.Chapter 36 promises not that God will help people try harder, but that he will give them a new heart. Where have you been leaning on willpower to change something that may actually need a change of heart you cannot manufacture?
2.Ezekiel watches the glory leave a temple where all the religious forms are still running. What is the practical difference between keeping the outward forms of faith and actually having God's presence among us — and how would you tell the two apart?
3.Think of a "valley of dry bones" situation in your own life or in this group — something that seems past recovery. Going by Ezekiel 37, what would it take for those bones to be assembled, and what would it take for them to actually breathe again?
4.True or False: A person can be faithfully keeping every religious practice and still be a place God's presence has quietly left.
Prayer
Father, we confess we cannot make ourselves new; only you can.
Take out our hearts of stone and give us hearts of flesh.
Put your Spirit within us, and breathe your life into what has died in us.
Come and dwell among us, so that we and others would know that you are the LORD.
Quote
"What comes into our minds when we think about God is the most important thing about us."
— A. W. Tozer, The Knowledge of the Holy
Memory Verse
Ezekiel 36:26 — "I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit in you; I will remove from you your heart of stone and give you a heart of flesh."
IV. Accountability
Break into small groups of 3–4 persons. Recite your four memory verses from the last four weeks. Share what you have done/learned in your daily devotion times. Share how you have sought opportunities to build relationships with unbelievers on your evangelism list, and the outcome of this.
V. Building Community
Do an exercise or have a discussion that builds stronger and deeper relationships. Make announcements and plans that affect the entire group.
VI. Closing
Close in prayer. Enjoy refreshments and fellowship.
