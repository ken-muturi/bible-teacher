The Sermon on the Mount — The Whole Charter of the Kingdom
Matthew 5–7
A combined overview session · pairs with the individual guides for chapters 5, 6 & 7
I. Connecting
Share what happened in your lives during the last week. Use an ice-breaker or other approach to get to know each other better.
II. Seeking God
(If your group is larger than 8 persons, break into groups of 3–4 for this.) Discuss any personal roadblocks, challenges, or fears that threaten your ability to walk by faith. Pray about things that will really make a difference in your life today.
III. Lesson Time
Ch 5 · Character
Beatitudes, salt & light, the higher righteousness of the heart
Ch 6 · Heart
Devotion, the Lord's Prayer, treasure & freedom from worry
Ch 7 · Response
Judging, ask-seek-knock, the two ways — hearing & doing
Observation
1.The Sermon opens with the Beatitudes (5:3–12) and closes with the two builders (7:24–27). What kind of person does it begin by calling "blessed," and what does it finally require of that person? How do the two bookends fit together?
2.Twice Jesus says a statement sums up "the Law and the Prophets" — 5:17 (he came to fulfil them) and 7:12 (the Golden Rule). What sits between those two markers, and what might that framing be telling us about the whole Sermon?
3.Track two repeated words across the three chapters: "Father" and "kingdom (of heaven)." Where do they appear, and how would you say the Sermon defines the good life differently from the world around it?
4.Chapter 5 goes after the heart behind the law, chapter 6 after the motive behind our devotion, chapter 7 after our response to Jesus' words. What single concern runs through all three — the difference between the outside and the inside of a life?
Application
1.Of the three movements — character (ch 5), heart before God (ch 6), and obedient response (ch 7) — which one is God pressing on you most right now, and what is he asking you to do about it?
2.The Sermon sets a standard so high (anger is murder, lust is adultery, love your enemies, be perfect) that no one can climb it by willpower. How does that impossible height actually push you toward grace rather than despair?
3.People admire the Sermon on the Mount more than almost any other text — and obey it least. Where are you most tempted to be an admirer of Jesus' words rather than a doer of them?
4.True or False: The Sermon on the Mount is a ladder of good behaviour we climb to earn God's acceptance.
Prayer
Father, we come as the poor in spirit — with nothing to offer but need.
Make us hunger for the righteousness only you can give.
Reach past our outward lives and change our hearts before you.
And keep us from admiring these words while failing to do them — build our house on the rock.
Quote
"The Christian ideal has not been tried and found wanting; it has been found difficult and left untried."
— G. K. Chesterton, What's Wrong with the World
Memory Verse
Matthew 5:6 — "Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they will be filled."
IV. Accountability
Break into small groups of 3–4 persons. Recite your memory verses from the last few weeks. Share what you have done/learned in your daily devotion times. Share how you have sought opportunities to build relationships with unbelievers on your evangelism list, and the outcome of this.
V. Building Community
Do an exercise or have a discussion that builds stronger and deeper relationships. Make announcements and plans that affect the entire group.
VI. Closing
Close in prayer. Enjoy refreshments and fellowship.
