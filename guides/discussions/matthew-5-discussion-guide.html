Kingdom Righteousness: The Sermon on the Mount Begins
Matthew 5
I. Connecting
Share what happened in your lives during the last week. Use an ice-breaker or other approach to get to know each other better.
II. Seeking God
(If your group is larger than 8 persons, break into groups of 3–4 for this.) Discuss any personal roadblocks, challenges, or fears that threaten your ability to walk by faith. Pray about things that will really make a difference in your life today.
III. Lesson Time
Observation
1.Read the Beatitudes (5:3–12). List the kinds of people Jesus calls "blessed." What do the poor in spirit, the mourners, the meek, and the persecuted have in common, and how does that list compare with who the world calls fortunate?
2.Jesus calls his disciples "salt of the earth" and "light of the world" (5:13–16). What specific danger does he name for each — what happens to salt, and to a lamp — and what does that warn against?
3.In 5:17–20 Jesus says he came not to abolish the Law and the Prophets but to "fulfil" them. What does he say must be true of his disciples' righteousness (v.20), and whose righteousness does he set as the bar to exceed?
4.Six times Jesus says "You have heard that it was said… but I say to you" (5:21–48). Pick two of the six (anger, lust, divorce, oaths, retaliation, enemies). In each, how does Jesus move the command from the outward act to something deeper?
Application
1.The Beatitudes describe a character that is the opposite of what our culture calls strong or successful. Which single beatitude is hardest for you to actually believe is "blessed," and why?
2.Where in your life is your "saltiness" or "light" most at risk right now — of losing its distinct flavour, or of being quietly hidden so no one sees it? What would it look like to let it "shine before others" this week?
3.Jesus traces murder back to anger and adultery back to lust — the heart, not just the hands. Where do you tend to settle for outward compliance while leaving the heart behind it unaddressed?
4.True or False: Jesus taught the Sermon on the Mount to lower the Old Testament's moral bar and make obedience easier.
Prayer
Father, we confess we are poor in spirit — we bring you empty hands.
Make us hunger and thirst for the righteousness only you can give.
Reach past our outward behaviour and change our hearts.
Make us salt and light, so that others would see and glorify you.
Quote
"If anyone will piously and soberly consider the sermon which our Lord Jesus Christ spoke on the mount, he will find in it a perfect standard of the Christian life."
— Augustine, Our Lord's Sermon on the Mount
Memory Verse
Matthew 5:16 — "Let your light shine before others, that they may see your good deeds and glorify your Father in heaven."
IV. Accountability
Break into small groups of 3–4 persons. Recite your memory verses from the last few weeks. Share what you have done/learned in your daily devotion times. Share how you have sought opportunities to build relationships with unbelievers on your evangelism list, and the outcome of this.
V. Building Community
Do an exercise or have a discussion that builds stronger and deeper relationships. Make announcements and plans that affect the entire group.
VI. Closing
Close in prayer. Enjoy refreshments and fellowship.
