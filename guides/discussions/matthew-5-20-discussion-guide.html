A Righteousness That Exceeds
Matthew 5:20
I. Connecting
Share what happened in your lives during the last week. Use an ice-breaker or other approach to get to know each other better.
II. Seeking God
(If your group is larger than 8 persons, break into groups of 3–4 for this.) Discuss any personal roadblocks, challenges, or fears that threaten your ability to walk by faith. Pray about things that will really make a difference in your life today.
III. Lesson Time
Observation
1.Read Matthew 5:20. Jesus says our righteousness must "exceed that of the scribes and Pharisees." Why is that shocking, given the Pharisees were the most careful rule-keepers of the day? Is Jesus asking for more of the same, or something different?
2.Read the "you have heard it said… but I say" statements in 5:21–22 and 5:27–28. Where does Jesus locate real righteousness — in the outward act, or somewhere deeper? What does that mean for "clean hands but an angry (or lustful) heart"?
3.Read Luke 18:9–14, the Pharisee and the tax collector. What does each man trust in — and which one "went down to his house justified"? Where does the greater righteousness begin?
4.Read Matthew 9:10–13. Jesus eats with sinners and quotes "I desire mercy, not sacrifice." How does Jesus' welcome of sinners differ from the Pharisees' separation — and what does that reveal about the two kinds of righteousness?
Application
1.Which is your subtler danger — obvious sin, or respectable religion that manages the outside while the heart stays proud? Be honest with each other.
2.The Pharisees' holiness pulled them away from sinners; Jesus' holiness moved toward them. Are you becoming a warmer or a more distant person toward "sinners"? Who have you quietly kept at arm's length?
3.Where is your obedience mostly external — right actions, wrong heart (anger, lust, contempt, comparison)? Name one area where Jesus is pressing past your hands to your heart.
4.The tax collector prayed, "God, be merciful to me, a sinner." Is that still your daily posture — or have you drifted toward "thank you that I'm not like other people"?
Prayer
Father, save us from a respectable righteousness that never reaches the heart.
Give us the greater righteousness that begins at the tax collector's cry for mercy.
Search our motives — our anger, lust, pride, and comparison — and change us from the inside.
And make us people that sinners find safe to draw near, as they did to Jesus.
Quote
"We are more sinful and flawed than we ever dared believe, yet more loved and accepted than we ever dared hope."
— Timothy Keller
Memory Verse
Matthew 5:20 — "For I tell you, unless your righteousness exceeds that of the scribes and Pharisees, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven."
IV. Accountability
Break into small groups of 3–4 persons. Recite your memory verses from the last few weeks. Share what you have done/learned in your daily devotion times. Share how you have sought opportunities to build relationships with unbelievers on your evangelism list, and the outcome of this.
V. Building Community
Do an exercise or have a discussion that builds stronger and deeper relationships. Make announcements and plans that affect the entire group.
VI. Closing
Close in prayer. Enjoy refreshments and fellowship.
