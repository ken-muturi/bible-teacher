How Beautiful Are Your Tents
Numbers 22–24 · Balaam's Oracle
I. Connecting
Share what happened in your lives during the last week. Use an ice-breaker or other approach to get to know each other better.
II. Seeking God
(If your group is larger than 8 persons, break into groups of 3–4 for this.) Discuss any personal roadblocks, challenges, or fears that threaten your ability to walk by faith. Pray about things that will really make a difference in your life today.
III. Lesson Time
Observation
1.Read the setting in Numbers 22:1–6, then 23:11 and 24:10. King Balak hires Balaam to curse Israel. What happens every single time Balaam opens his mouth — and who, then, is really in control of the prophet's words?
2.Read Balaam's third oracle, Numbers 24:5–9. What does he see when he looks down on Israel's camp, and what images does he reach for (gardens, cedars, water)? Notice how 24:9 quotes the promise to Abraham (Gen 12:3) — what is God enforcing here?
3.Numbers 23:21 says God sees "no iniquity in Jacob" — yet Israel sins grievously in the very next chapter (ch. 25, Baal-Peor). How can both be true? What must Israel's "beauty" be resting on, if not their performance?
4.Read Numbers 24:17 — "a star shall come out of Jacob." Who do Jewish and Christian readers see this pointing toward? How does Matthew 2:1–2 pick up the image of a star and a king?
Application
1.Balak spent a fortune trying to curse what God had blessed — and failed every time. Where do you live in fear that some enemy, failure, or "curse" could undo what God has spoken over you? What does this passage say to that fear?
2.Israel's beauty rested on God dwelling among them and his promise — even as they were about to fail. How does that reframe your standing before God on a day when you've sinned and feel unlovely?
3.The man hired to curse ended up blessing. Where might God be working for your good even through hostile or unlikely circumstances you can't yet see?
4.The real danger wasn't the curse from outside (which failed) but the seduction into sin from within (Baal-Peor, ch. 25). Where are you more exposed to temptation than to opposition — and how will you guard against it this week?
Prayer
Father, thank you that what you have blessed, no one can curse.
Thank you that our beauty rests on your presence and your promise, not on our record.
Guard us not only from the enemy's opposition but from his seduction into sin.
And thank you for the Star that rose out of Jacob — the King in whom every blessing is secured.
Quote
"God's promises are like the stars; the darker the night, the brighter they shine."
— Charles H. Spurgeon
Memory Verse
Numbers 23:19 — "God is not man, that he should lie, or a son of man, that he should change his mind. Has he said, and will he not do it?"
IV. Accountability
Break into small groups of 3–4 persons. Recite your memory verses from the last few weeks. Share what you have done/learned in your daily devotion times. Share how you have sought opportunities to build relationships with unbelievers on your evangelism list, and the outcome of this.
V. Building Community
Do an exercise or have a discussion that builds stronger and deeper relationships. Make announcements and plans that affect the entire group.
VI. Closing
Close in prayer. Enjoy refreshments and fellowship.
