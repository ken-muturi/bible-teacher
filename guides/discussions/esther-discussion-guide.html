Esther: For Such a Time as This
The Book of Esther
I. Connecting
Share what happened in your lives during the last week. Use an ice-breaker or other approach to get to know each other better.
II. Seeking God
(If your group is larger than 8 persons, break into groups of 3–4 for this.) Discuss any personal roadblocks, challenges, or fears that threaten your ability to walk by faith. Pray about things that will really make a difference in your life today.
III. Lesson Time
Observation
1.Famously, God is never named in Esther — no prayer, law, or miracle is recorded. Yet read 4:14 and 6:1. Where do you sense God at work even though he is never mentioned? What might the book's very silence about God be teaching?
2.Read Esther 3:1–6 and 4:13–16. What is the threat against the Jews, and what does Mordecai say to Esther about her position? How does 4:14 hold together God's certainty ("relief will rise") and Esther's responsibility ("who knows…")?
3.The book turns on reversals (read 9:1). Trace a few: the gallows Haman built (5:14 → 7:10), the king's sleepless night (6:1), mourning turned to feasting (9:22). Who is quietly turning the tables — and how?
4.Read Esther 4:15–16. What does Esther decide, and what does "if I perish, I perish" reveal about her faith and courage? Notice she calls a fast before she acts — why does that order matter?
Application
1.Most of life is lived in "Esther's world" — no thunder, no burning bush, just decisions and "coincidences." Where is it hard for you to trust that God is at work when you can neither see nor hear him?
2."For such a time as this." Where has God placed you — this family, job, city, friendship, moment — and is there a courageous step you've been avoiding? What is holding you back?
3.Esther fasted before she acted. When you face something daunting, do you move straight to action, or seek God first? What would "seek God, then act" look like for you this week?
4.Esther risked her comfort and safety for the good of others. Where might God be calling you to spend your position, privilege, or voice for someone else's sake — even at a cost to yourself?
Prayer
Father, thank you that even when we cannot see or hear you, you are never absent and never idle.
Help us trust your hidden hand in the "coincidences" and decisions of ordinary life.
Show us where you have placed us "for such a time as this," and give us Esther's courage.
Teach us to seek you first — and then to step out, trusting that your purpose is safe.
Quote
"Behind a frowning providence He hides a smiling face."
— William Cowper, "God Moves in a Mysterious Way"
Memory Verse
Esther 4:14 — "…And who knows whether you have not come to the kingdom for such a time as this?"
IV. Accountability
Break into small groups of 3–4 persons. Recite your memory verses from the last few weeks. Share what you have done/learned in your daily devotion times. Share how you have sought opportunities to build relationships with unbelievers on your evangelism list, and the outcome of this.
V. Building Community
Do an exercise or have a discussion that builds stronger and deeper relationships. Make announcements and plans that affect the entire group.
VI. Closing
Close in prayer. Enjoy refreshments and fellowship.
