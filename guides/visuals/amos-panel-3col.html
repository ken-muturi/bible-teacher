The Confrontation — 7:10–17 Amaziah (priest of Bethel) reports Amos to King Jeroboam and orders him home. Amos refuses — his authority is divine call, not institutional permission. The live question: who gets to speak prophetically, and by whose authority?
Hard Question Is Amos 9:11–15 (the sudden restoration promise) original? Many scholars argue it was added later to soften the book. Others defend it. Name both positions — don't pretend it isn't live. [VERIFY: Shalom Paul or James Mays]
Key Words
מִשְׁפָּט / Mishpat (Justice)
Active restoration of right community relationship — not just avoiding wrong. "Let justice roll down like waters." (5:24)
צְדָקָה / Tzedakah (Righteousness)
Rightly ordered relationship — outward, social. Always paired with mishpat. Together: what God actually requires of a community.
יוֹם יְהוָה / Yom YHWH (Day of the LORD)
Israel expected a victory parade. Amos flips it: "It is darkness, not light." (5:18) One of the earliest uses — and the sharpest inversion.
שְׁאָנַן / Sha'anan (At ease / complacent)
"At ease in Zion" (6:1). Not active evil — settled indifference. Obliviousness to what is happening around you.

Then → Now
The Indictment That Travels Amos doesn't let you choose between orthodoxy and justice. Israel was theologically careful and economically indifferent — and God called it an insult. Religious communities today can be simultaneously articulate about belief and inattentive to the neighbor.
What Amos Uniquely Contributes
Worship that bypasses the poor is not worship of the God of Israel. It is something else wearing his name.
The standard every prophet after Amos inherits

Question to Sit With
Amos says God despises worship disconnected from how worshippers treat the vulnerable. What would it mean for a community — not as a program, but as a definition — to take that seriously?
