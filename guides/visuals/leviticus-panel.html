The Five Offerings (Chs. 1–7)
Burnt Offering עֹלָה · Olah · "that which goes up" Entirely consumed. Total dedication — nothing kept by the offerer.
Grain Offering מִנְחָה · Minchah · "gift / tribute" Flour, oil, incense. Accompanies animal offerings. Acknowledges God as provider.
Peace Offering שְׁלָמִים · Shelamim · "wholeness" Shared meal: parts for God, parts for priest, parts for worshipper. Communion.
Sin Offering חַטָּאת · Chatat · "to miss the mark" Addresses unintentional sin or impurity. Restores access to the holy space.
Guilt Offering אָשָׁם · Asham · "liability / debt" For specific violations. Includes restitution — repair, not just forgiveness.
Each offering addresses a different aspect of the human–God relationship
The Hinge — Ch. 16: Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur)
Goat 1 — Sacrificed Blood brought into the Most Holy Place. Propitiation — the holiness of God is satisfied.
Goat 2 — Azazel (Scapegoat) Sins confessed over it, driven into the wilderness. Expiation — guilt is removed and sent away.
Two goats, two problems: the presence of holiness + the reality of sin · Once a year · High priest only
Key Passage — Read Aloud
"You shall be holy, for I the LORD your God am holy."
Leviticus 19:2 (ESV) — the Holiness Code's opening command
Hardest Moment — Ch. 10: Nadab & Abihu Aaron's sons offer "unauthorized fire" and are immediately killed. Not arbitrary cruelty — the point is that proximity to holiness is not casual. The extensive ordination ritual that precedes this existed precisely because the stakes were this high.
Key Words
קָדוֹשׁ / Qadosh (Holy)
Appears 152× in Leviticus. "Set apart / other / categorically different." Ontological, not just moral — God is holy because he is unlike everything else.
כָּפַר / Kaphar (Atonement)
"To cover / ransom / wipe clean." Root of Yom Kippur. Closer to satisfying a debt than simply forgiving it.
טָמֵא / Tame (Unclean)
Ritual impurity — a state, not a sin. Being tamei is not morally culpable; it means you cannot approach the holy space until resolved.

What This Book Uniquely Contributes
Holiness has a cost. Mediation is necessary. You cannot simply walk into the presence of the holy — something has to be done first.
