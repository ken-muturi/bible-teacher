ISRAEL
The longest oracle. The real target. The trap is closed.
"For three transgressions of X, and for four, I will not revoke the punishment." — repeated 8 times. The audience cheered through six nations. Shifted uneasily at Judah. The trap closed on them.
What Israel Was Doing
Selling the righteous for silver, the needy for a pair of sandals (2:6)
Trampling the head of the poor into the dust (2:7)
Elaborate religious festivals — sacrifice, offerings, music (5:21–23)
Lounging on ivory beds, eating choice lambs, drinking wine (6:4–6)
Women of Samaria ("cows of Bashan") oppressing the poor and crushing the needy (4:1)
"At ease in Zion" — not grieving over the ruin of Joseph (6:1, 6)
What God Actually Required
"Seek me and live" — but not at Bethel or Gilgal (5:4–6)
"Seek good and not evil, that you may live" (5:14)
"I hate, I despise your feasts... take away from me the noise of your songs" (5:21–23)
Hate evil, love good, establish justice in the gate (5:15)
Amos 9:7 — God moves all nations, not just Israel. Covenant ≠ immunity.
The Exodus did not give Israel a permanent advantage — it gave them a permanent responsibility.
Key Passage — Read Aloud  ·  Amos 5:21–24
"I hate, I despise your feasts... But let justice roll down like waters, and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream."
Amos 5:21, 24 (ESV) — the most quoted verse in the book, and the one that defines the whole argument
Who Amos Was
Sheep breeder (noqed) and sycamore fig farmer from Tekoa, Judah. A southerner sent north. Not a professional prophet.
"I am not a prophet, nor a son of a prophet, but I am a herdsman... But the LORD took me from following the flock." — 7:14–15
The Confrontation — 7:10–17
Amaziah (priest of Bethel) reports Amos to King Jeroboam as a conspirator and tells him to go back to Judah. Amos refuses. His authority is a divine call — not an institution's permission. The question it raises: who gets to speak prophetically, and by whose authority?
Five Visions — Chs. 7–9  ·  Escalating severity  ·  The first two God relents; from the third, no more
1
Relents Locusts 7:1–3
A swarm about to devour the land. Amos intercedes: "O Lord, please forgive!" God relents.
Outcome: "It shall not be." (7:3)
2
Relents Fire 7:4–6
A consuming fire devouring the deep and the land. Amos intercedes again. God relents again.
Outcome: "This also shall not be." (7:6)
3
No Relenting Plumb Line 7:7–9
God holds a plumb line against Israel. The standard is set; the wall is out of true. No intercession this time.
"I will never again pass by them." (7:8) [VERIFY: anak = plumb line?]
4
Wordplay Summer Fruit 8:1–3
Qaitz (summer fruit) sounds like qetz (end). The harvest is ripe — for Israel's end.
"The end has come upon my people Israel." (8:2)
5
No Escape God at the Altar 9:1–4
God stands at the altar commanding destruction. Sheol, heaven, sea — nowhere to hide. No intercession possible.
"I will fix my eyes upon them for evil and not for good." (9:4)
Key Words
מִשְׁפָּט / Mishpat (Justice)
"Let justice roll down like waters" (5:24). Not legal correctness — the active restoration of right community relationship. Requires doing, not just avoiding.
צְדָקָה / Tzedakah (Righteousness)
Rightly ordered relationship — outward-facing, social. Always paired with mishpat. Together they describe what God requires of a community.
יוֹם יְהוָה / Yom YHWH (Day of the LORD)
Israel expected a victory parade. Amos flips it: "It is darkness, not light." (5:18) One of the earliest uses in the prophets — and the sharpest inversion.
שְׁאָנַן / Sha'anan (Complacent)
"At ease in Zion" (6:1). Not active evil — comfortable indifference. The word implies settled obliviousness to what is actually happening around you.

Then → Now
The Indictment That Travels Amos does not let you choose between orthodoxy and justice. Israel was theologically careful and economically indifferent — and God called it an insult. Religious communities today can be simultaneously articulate about belief and inattentive to the neighbor. Amos insists these cannot be separated without destroying both.
Who Amos Preached To
Context Jeroboam II's reign — northern kingdom's peak prosperity. Archaeologists confirm: ivory artifacts, large houses alongside small ones, elaborate sanctuaries. This is a wealthy, religious, apparently successful nation.
What Amos Uniquely Contributes
Worship that bypasses the poor is not worship of the God of Israel. It is something else wearing his name.
The standard every prophet after Amos inherits
Period / Setting
~760–750 BC · reign of Jeroboam II · northern kingdom at peak prosperity · Amos preaches at Bethel, the royal sanctuary
Question to Sit With
Amos says God despises worship disconnected from how worshippers treat the vulnerable. What would it mean for a community — or a personal practice — to take that seriously, not as a program but as a definition?
