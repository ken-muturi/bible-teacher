From Pentecost to the Jerusalem Council
The first twenty years of the church — Acts 1–15
Span: ~AD 30 (the Spirit poured out) → ~AD 48–49 (the Council settles the Gentile question). A single generation in which a Jerusalem sect became a movement reaching the nations — and had to decide, from Scripture, whether Gentiles must become Jews to be saved.
How to Read This
Phase 1 · The church is born in Jerusalem
Phase 2 · The gospel breaks out to the nations
Phase 3 · Mission & the Council
A note on dates: New Testament chronology is approximate and debated. The crucifixion is dated to AD 30 or 33; the Council to AD 48, 49, or 50. The firm anchor is the death of Herod Agrippa I in AD 44 (Acts 12:23), fixed by Roman records — everything else is spaced around it. Dates below are the commonly used approximations.
Phase 1 — The Church Is Born (Jerusalem)
~AD 30/33
Acts 1
Resurrection, Ascension & the Waiting
The risen Jesus teaches for forty days, commissions the apostles as witnesses "to the ends of the earth," and ascends. The 120 wait and pray in Jerusalem; Matthias replaces Judas.
~AD 30/33
Pentecost
Acts 2
The Spirit Poured Out — the Church Born
At Pentecost the Holy Spirit falls; Peter preaches Joel's prophecy fulfilled; about 3,000 are baptised. The church begins as a Spirit-filled, teaching, sharing, worshipping community.
~AD 30–33
Acts 3–5
Growth, Signs & First Opposition
Peter heals the lame man; thousands more believe. The apostles are hauled before the Sanhedrin — "we must obey God rather than men." Ananias & Sapphira; Gamaliel's counsel of restraint.
~AD 33–34
Acts 6–7
Stephen — the First Martyr
Seven are chosen to serve; Stephen, full of the Spirit, is seized and stoned after his sweeping speech. "Saul approved of his execution." A great persecution breaks out against the Jerusalem church.
Phase 2 — The Gospel Breaks Out
~AD 34
Acts 8
Scattered — Samaria & the Ethiopian
Persecution scatters believers, and they preach wherever they go. Philip brings the gospel to Samaria and to an Ethiopian court official on the desert road — the first steps beyond Judea.
~AD 33–34
Acts 9 · Gal 1
The Conversion of Saul
On the road to Damascus, the risen Christ confronts Saul the persecutor. Blinded, then baptised, he begins preaching Jesus. The church's fiercest enemy becomes its apostle to the Gentiles.
~AD 34–37
Gal 1:17–18 · Acts 9:26–30
Arabia, Damascus & a First Jerusalem Visit
Saul spends time in Arabia and Damascus, escapes over the wall in a basket, meets Peter and James briefly in Jerusalem, then is sent home to Tarsus — years of preparation out of the spotlight.
~AD 38–40
Acts 10–11
Peter & Cornelius — the Spirit on Gentiles
Through a vision and a Roman centurion, God gives his Spirit to uncircumcised Gentiles. Peter: "God shows no partiality." The Jerusalem church, astonished, concludes God has "granted repentance to the Gentiles."
~AD 40–44
Acts 11:19–26
Antioch — "Christians" & a Gentile Church
In Antioch the gospel is preached to Greeks and a great mixed church forms. Barnabas fetches Saul from Tarsus; for a year they teach there. The disciples are first called "Christians."
Phase 3 — Mission & the Council
AD 44
(anchor)
Acts 12
Herod Agrippa — James Killed, Peter Freed
Agrippa I executes James (son of Zebedee) and imprisons Peter, who is freed by an angel. Struck down for accepting worship, Agrippa dies — a fixed date (AD 44) that anchors the whole chronology.
~AD 46–47
Acts 11:27–30 · 12:25
The Famine-Relief Visit to Jerusalem
Warned by the prophet Agabus of a famine, the Antioch church sends aid to Judea by Barnabas and Saul — an early picture of Gentile-background believers caring for the mother church.
~AD 46–48
Acts 13–14
The First Missionary Journey
Set apart by the Spirit, Barnabas and Saul (now Paul) sail to Cyprus and journey through Galatia — Pisidian Antioch, Iconium, Lystra, Derbe — planting churches, turning to the Gentiles, and appointing elders.
~AD 48–49
Acts 15:1–5 · Galatians
The Crisis: "Unless You Are Circumcised…"
Men from Judea insist Gentile converts must be circumcised and keep the law of Moses to be saved. The gospel of grace is at stake. (Galatians is likely written into this same fight.) Paul and Barnabas are sent to Jerusalem.
~AD 48/49
Acts 15:6–29 — the destination
The Jerusalem Council
Peter testifies, Paul and Barnabas report, and James settles it from Scripture (Amos 9): Gentiles are saved by grace through faith, not by the law. The church's greatest controversy is decided by the Word — and the door to the nations stands open.
Who's Who in These Years
Peter
Lead apostle
Preaches at Pentecost, opens the door to Cornelius's household, and defends Gentile inclusion at the Council.
Stephen
First martyr
A Spirit-filled deacon whose death ignites the persecution that scatters the gospel beyond Jerusalem.
Philip
Evangelist
Carries the gospel to Samaria and to the Ethiopian official — the first cross-cultural mission.
Saul / Paul
Apostle to the Gentiles
Persecutor turned missionary; converted on the Damascus road, launched from Antioch, defender of grace at the Council.
Barnabas
Encourager, mentor
Vouches for Saul, leads the Antioch work, and partners on the first journey — the bridge between Jerusalem and the Gentile mission.
James (the Lord's brother)
Leader, Jerusalem church
Pronounces the decisive judgment at the Council, rooting the decision in the prophets (Amos 9).
James (son of Zebedee)
Apostle, martyr AD 44
Executed by Herod Agrippa I — the first apostle to die (Acts 12).
Cornelius
Roman centurion
The first Gentile convert to receive the Spirit — the test case God used to open the church to the nations.
Herod Agrippa I
King (d. AD 44)
Persecutor whose sudden death gives the era its one firmly fixed date.
Written in (or into) This Window
Galatians
~AD 48–49 (on the "South Galatian" view)
Many date Galatians to just before or around the Council — Paul's white-hot defence of justification by faith apart from the law, fighting the very error the Council would address. On other views it is slightly later.
James
~AD 44–49 (possibly the earliest NT letter)
Often dated early, before the Council — a practical letter from James of Jerusalem to Jewish believers scattered abroad, reading much like the Sermon on the Mount applied.
The Shape of Twenty Years
"In a single generation the gospel moved from an upper room in Jerusalem to churches across the Roman world — pushed outward by persecution, carried by a converted enemy, and confirmed for the nations at a council that decided the question by Scripture."
Acts 1:8 → Acts 15 — "you will be my witnesses… to the ends of the earth."
