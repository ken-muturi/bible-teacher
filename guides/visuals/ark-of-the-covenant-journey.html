The Ark of the Covenant

Its journey — from Mount Sinai to the silence of history
The ark was a gold-covered acacia chest crowned by the mercy seat and two cherubim — the throne of God's presence among Israel, holding the tablets of the Law (and, Hebrews 9:4 adds, the jar of manna and Aaron's staff). For some five centuries it travels through Israel's story; then, after the fall of Jerusalem, it simply disappears from the record. Here is where it went — and where the trail goes cold. (Dates are traditional and approximate.)
Exodus & Wilderness Conquest & Judges United Kingdom Temple & Divided Kingdom Exile — the trail ends
The Journey — Fifteen Stations
01
Made at Mount Sinai
~1446 BC
God gives the pattern; Bezalel builds it — acacia wood overlaid with pure gold, the mercy seat and two cherubim on top. It becomes the meeting place: "there… I will meet with you" (Ex 25:22). The tablets of the covenant are placed inside.
Exodus 25:10–22Exodus 37:1–9Hebrews 9:4
02
Leading Through the Wilderness
~1446–1406 BC
Carried by the Kohathite Levites on poles (never to be touched), the ark goes ahead of Israel to seek out a resting place. When it set out Moses cried, "Rise up, LORD!" — the ark as the moving throne of God in the camp.
Numbers 4:5–15Numbers 10:33–36
03
Parting the Jordan
~1406 BC
As the priests carrying the ark step into the flooded Jordan, the waters pile up and Israel crosses on dry ground. The ark stands in the riverbed until all have passed — a second Red Sea, led by the throne of God.
Joshua 3:14–17Joshua 4:1–11
04
Circling Jericho
~1406 BC
The ark is carried around Jericho once a day for six days, and seven times on the seventh — and the walls fall. Victory comes not by siege engines but by the presence of God borne around the city.
Joshua 6:1–20
05
Resting at Shiloh
~1400–1050 BC
After the conquest the tabernacle — and the ark — settle at Shiloh, Israel's central sanctuary for roughly three centuries through the era of the judges. Here Hannah prays and Samuel grows up ministering before the LORD.
Joshua 18:1Judges 20:271 Samuel 1–3
06
Captured by the Philistines
~1050 BC
Israel drags the ark into battle at Ebenezer as a good-luck charm. It backfires: 30,000 fall, Eli's sons Hophni and Phinehas die, the ark is captured, and Eli dies at the news. A dying mother names her son Ichabod — "the glory has departed."
1 Samuel 4:1–22
07
Seven Months in Philistia
~1050 BC
In Ashdod the idol Dagon falls face-down before the ark and breaks apart. Tumors and terror strike Ashdod, Gath, and Ekron in turn. The "captured" ark needs no army — it devastates its captors until they beg to send it home.
1 Samuel 5:1–12
08
Returned to Beth-Shemesh
~1050 BC
The Philistines send it back on a new cart with a guilt offering of gold. Israel rejoices — but men of Beth-Shemesh look into the ark and are struck down. Holiness is not sentimental; the throne of God is not to be handled casually.
1 Samuel 6:1–21
09
Twenty Years at Kiriath-Jearim
~1050–1000 BC
The ark is lodged in the house of Abinadab on the hill, with his son Eleazar consecrated to guard it. It stays there, largely neglected, through the rest of Samuel's judgeship and into Saul's reign — out of sight, out of mind.
1 Samuel 7:1–21 Chronicles 13:3
10
Uzzah and the House of Obed-Edom
~1000 BC
David tries to bring the ark to Jerusalem — but on a cart, the Philistine way, not on the Levites' shoulders. When it tips and Uzzah steadies it, he is struck dead. A frightened David leaves it at Obed-Edom's house for three months, where it brings blessing.
2 Samuel 6:1–111 Chronicles 15:13–15
11
David Brings It to Jerusalem
~1000 BC
Carried properly by the Levites this time, the ark enters Jerusalem with sacrifice and shouting as David dances before it "with all his might." He pitches a tent for it on Zion — the city of David becomes the city of God's throne.
2 Samuel 6:12–191 Chronicles 15–16Psalm 132
12
Kept in the City Through Absalom's Revolt
~975 BC
When Absalom rebels and David flees, the priests carry the ark out to go with him — but David sends it back: "If I find favor… he will bring me back." He will not use the ark as a talisman. It stays in Jerusalem, awaiting a permanent home.
2 Samuel 15:24–29
13
Installed in Solomon's Temple
~960 BC
Solomon places the ark in the Most Holy Place, beneath the wings of two great cherubim. The glory-cloud fills the house. Inside the ark now, only the two tablets remain. "And there it is to this day," notes the historian — the ark at rest at last.
1 Kings 8:1–112 Chronicles 5:2–10
14
Josiah's Last Word
~622 BC
During his reforms, King Josiah tells the Levites to "put the sacred ark in the temple… It is not to be carried about on your shoulders." This is the last clear historical mention of the physical ark — implying it had been moved or stored during the dark years of Manasseh.
2 Chronicles 35:3
15
Silence — The Fall of Jerusalem
586 BC
Babylon burns the temple and carries off its treasures — the bronze, the pillars, the vessels are all itemized (2 Kings 25; Jer 52). But the ark is never mentioned: not in the plunder lists, not among the articles returned under Cyrus (Ezra 1). The Second Temple's Most Holy Place stood empty. From here, the ark vanishes from history.
2 Kings 25:8–17Jeremiah 52:17–23Ezra 1:7–11
Where Did It Go? — The Main Theories
1. Destroyed or carried off by Babylon (586 BC)
Most common scholarly view
The simplest reading: the ark was looted or burned when Nebuchadnezzar destroyed Solomon's temple. Its absence from the detailed plunder lists is taken as a sign it was already gone, melted for its gold, or not worth separate mention.
2. Hidden by Jeremiah before the fall
2 Maccabees 2:4–8 · deuterocanonical
A tradition says the prophet Jeremiah, warned by God, hid the ark (with the altar of incense) in a cave on Mount Nebo, sealing the entrance, to stay hidden "until God gathers his people again." Found in 2 Maccabees — Scripture for Catholics/Orthodox, not in the Protestant canon.
3. Hidden under the temple mount
Rabbinic tradition
Several rabbinic sources hold that Josiah, foreseeing the exile, concealed the ark in a secret chamber beneath the temple, where it remains. A variant has it buried deep below the Holy of Holies.
4. Taken to Egypt earlier
Speculative
Some connect its disappearance to Pharaoh Shishak's raid on Jerusalem (~925 BC), when "the treasures of the temple" were carried off (1 Kings 14:25–26). The text never names the ark, so this is inference, not record.
5. In Axum, Ethiopia
Ethiopian Orthodox tradition (Kebra Nagast)
The Ethiopian Church claims the true ark rests in the Church of Our Lady Mary of Zion in Axum, guarded by a single monk, brought there by Menelik, son of Solomon and the Queen of Sheba. Devotional tradition, not historically verifiable.
The Last Words on the Ark
2 Chronicles 35:3 · ~622 BC
"Put the sacred ark in the temple… It is not to be carried about on your shoulders."
The last historical mention of the physical ark — and Chronicles is the final book of the Hebrew Bible.
Jeremiah 3:16 · ~600 BC
"People will no longer say, 'The ark of the covenant of the LORD'… nor will it be missed."
A prophecy that the ark would one day be gone — and that it would not matter, because God himself would be present.
Hebrews 9:4 · NT
"…the gold-covered ark of the covenant. This ark contained the gold jar of manna, Aaron's staff… and the stone tablets."
The New Testament describes the ark looking back — explaining the old covenant tabernacle as a shadow of Christ.
Revelation 11:19 · the final mention
"God's temple in heaven was opened, and within his temple was seen the ark of his covenant."
The very last reference in the Bible — not the lost golden box, but the heavenly ark it only ever copied. The covenant presence, secure in heaven.
The Thread
The earthly ark's trail ends in the smoke of 586 BC; its last historical word is Josiah's in 2 Chronicles. But the Bible's final word on the ark is Revelation 11:19 — pointing past the lost chest to the covenant presence of God, kept safe in heaven and opened at the end.
