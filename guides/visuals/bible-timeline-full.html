Contemporaries — Who Was Alive at the Same Time
Antediluvian
Adam & Methuselah
They were alive at the same time
Adam lived 930 years. Methuselah was born when Adam was 687 years old (AM 687). They overlapped by 243 years — Methuselah almost certainly knew Adam personally and heard the account of Eden firsthand.
Pre-Flood
Methuselah & Noah
They were alive at the same time
Methuselah died in AM 1656 — the very year of the flood. As Noah's grandfather, he lived to see the ark under construction. He died either just before or as the waters rose.
Post-Flood Patriarchs
Abraham & Shem
They were alive at the same time
Shem (son of Noah) lived 600 years and outlived Abraham by 35 years. Abraham could have met the man who survived the flood. Some traditions identify Shem with Melchizedek, King of Salem.
Patriarchs
Joseph & Levi
They were alive at the same time
Joseph and his brother Levi were contemporaries. The founding of the Levitical priesthood line overlaps directly with Joseph's years in Egypt. Both were in Egypt at the same time.
Exodus
Moses & Joshua
They were alive at the same time
Joshua served as Moses' aide from his youth (Exod 33:11). Moses mentored Joshua across forty years of wilderness wandering, then commissioned him to complete what Moses was not permitted to finish — entering the land.
Judges–Kingdom
David & Samuel
They were alive at the same time
Samuel anointed David as king while Saul still reigned (1 Sam 16). Their overlap is the precise hinge between the era of judges and the era of the monarchy. Samuel died before David's full coronation.
Divided Kingdom
Isaiah & Hezekiah
They were alive at the same time
Isaiah prophesied throughout Hezekiah's reign and his prophecies were fulfilled in real time — including the miraculous retreat of Sennacherib and fifteen additional years added to Hezekiah's life (Isa 38).
Exile
Jeremiah & Daniel
They were alive at the same time
Both were alive during the fall of Jerusalem in 586 BC. Jeremiah remained in Jerusalem weeping (Lamentations). Daniel had already been taken to Babylon in 605 BC and served in Nebuchadnezzar's court.
Post-Exile
Ezra & Nehemiah
They were alive at the same time
Ezra and Nehemiah overlapped in Jerusalem during the reconstruction era. Ezra arrived first (~458 BC) to restore the Law. Nehemiah arrived ~445 BC to rebuild the walls. Their ministries are complementary and simultaneous.
Books in Context — When Was Each Era's Scripture Written?
Antediluvian
~4004–2348 BC · AM 1–1656
Genesis 1–11
No canonical books written during this era. Genesis 1–11 covers it retrospectively — Moses wrote the account around 1450 BC based on oral tradition and divine revelation.
Post-Flood Patriarchs
~2348–1800 BC
Genesis 12–50
Written by Moses ~1450 BC, covering the events of ~2000–1700 BC. The accounts of Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and Joseph likely preserved oral/written records.
Sojourn & Exodus
~1800–1406 BC
Exodus Leviticus Numbers Deuteronomy Job
Moses wrote these c. 1446–1406 BC. Job may be the oldest book by content, set in the patriarchal period, possibly written by Moses or Job himself.
Conquest & Judges
~1406–1050 BC
Joshua Judges Ruth
Joshua likely wrote the book bearing his name (with a final section by Eleazar or Phinehas). Judges and Ruth written or compiled by Samuel or a contemporary ~1050 BC.
United Kingdom
~1050–930 BC
1–2 Samuel 1 Kings 1–11 1 Chronicles Psalms (David) Proverbs Ecclesiastes Song of Solomon
David wrote most of the Psalms. Solomon authored Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, and Song of Solomon. Samuel began the historical narrative, completed by later prophets.
Divided Kingdom
~930–586 BC
1–2 Kings 2 Chronicles Isaiah Jeremiah Lamentations Hosea Joel Amos Obadiah Jonah Micah Nahum Habakkuk Zephaniah
The great prophetic literature — written by the named prophets during the era of Israel's and Judah's moral decline, exile, and judgment.
Exile
~605–538 BC
Daniel Ezekiel Lamentations
Daniel wrote from Babylon across 70 years of exile. Ezekiel prophesied to the exiles in Babylon. Lamentations is Jeremiah's lament over fallen Jerusalem.
Post-Exile & Return
~538–430 BC
Ezra Nehemiah Esther Haggai Zechariah Malachi
Malachi (~430 BC) closes the OT canon. After him, 400 years of prophetic silence follow until John the Baptist.
Intertestamental Period
~430 BC – ~5 BC
No canonical books written.
400 years of silence. The Apocrypha (Maccabees, Sirach, Tobit, etc.) were written during this era but are not accepted as canonical in Protestant tradition. Rome fell to Greece (Alexander), Greece to Rome — setting the stage for the New Testament world.
New Testament
~AD 45–100
Matthew Mark Luke John Acts Romans–Jude Revelation
27 books written by eyewitnesses and apostolic companions. Paul's letters predate the Gospels (~AD 48–65). John's Revelation (~AD 95) closes the canon.

15 Pivotal Events — The Spine of Biblical History
Creation AM 1 · ~4004 BC
God creates the heavens, earth, and humanity in six days. Adam and Eve placed in Eden. Time begins.
The Fall AM 1 · ~4004 BC
Adam and Eve disobey God. Sin, death, and exile from Eden enter human history. The proto-evangelium (Gen 3:15) — the first promise of a Redeemer — is spoken.
The Flood AM 1656 · ~2348 BC
God judges the earth with a global flood. Noah, his family, and the animals are preserved in the ark. Methuselah dies this year. A new covenant is made with Noah (Gen 9).
Tower of Babel ~2242 BC
Humanity unites in rebellion. God confuses languages and scatters the nations. The 70 nations of Genesis 10 disperse across the earth.
Call of Abraham AM 2083 · ~1921 BC
God calls Abram out of Ur of the Chaldees. The Abrahamic covenant is established — land, seed, and blessing to all nations (Gen 12, 15, 17). The messianic line narrows.
The Exodus ~1446 BC
God delivers Israel from 400 years of Egyptian bondage through Moses. Ten plagues, the Passover, and the crossing of the Red Sea. ~2–3 million people leave Egypt.
The Law Given at Sinai ~1446 BC
God gives the Ten Commandments and the Mosaic Law to Israel at Mount Sinai. The tabernacle is constructed. The sacrificial system is established — a shadow of the coming atonement.
David Anointed King ~1010 BC
Samuel anoints David king of Israel. The Davidic covenant is established (2 Sam 7) — promising an eternal throne and a coming son who will rule forever. This is the Messiah's title: Son of David.
Solomon's Temple Built ~966 BC
Solomon builds the First Temple in Jerusalem, fulfilling what David desired. The ark of the covenant is placed in the holy of holies. The glory of God fills the temple (1 Kgs 8).
Kingdom Divides ~930 BC
After Solomon's death, Rehoboam's harsh rule splits the kingdom. Israel (10 tribes, North) under Jeroboam. Judah (2 tribes, South) under Rehoboam. The divided kingdom era begins.
Fall of Samaria (Northern Kingdom) 722 BC
Assyria under Sargon II conquers the Northern Kingdom. The ten tribes are scattered — the "lost tribes." Fulfilled exactly as Amos and Hosea had prophesied.
Fall of Jerusalem (Southern Kingdom) 586 BC
Nebuchadnezzar destroys Jerusalem and Solomon's Temple. The Davidic dynasty is suspended. Judah is carried into Babylonian exile — the 70-year captivity prophesied by Jeremiah begins.
Return from Exile 538 BC
Cyrus the Great of Persia decrees that the Jews may return to Judah and rebuild the temple — fulfilling Isaiah's prophecy (Isa 44:28) written 150 years earlier, naming Cyrus by name.
Birth of Jesus ~6/4 BC
The eternal Son of God takes on human flesh in Bethlehem, born of the virgin Mary. Son of David. Son of Abraham. The 400-year silence ends. The angel declares: "For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord" (Luke 2:11).
Death & Resurrection of Jesus ~AD 30
Jesus is crucified at Passover — the final Passover Lamb. On the third day he rises bodily from the dead, conquering sin and death. The new covenant is inaugurated in his blood. History's pivot point.
