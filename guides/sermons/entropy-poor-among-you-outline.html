Sermon Outline
Fighting Entropy
"The poor you will always have with you" — and why poverty's opposite is not wealth but intention
Preaching Outline · 3 points
Text & Frame
Matthew 26:6–13 (the anointing at Bethany), framed by Deuteronomy 15:1–11 (the year of release)
Supporting
Mark 14:3–9 · John 12:1–8 · Gen 3:17–19 · Luke 15:11–24 · 2 Cor 8:9 · 1 Cor 15:42
Aim
To move hearers from passive drift to intentional stewardship, generosity, and discipleship — under the God who became poor to make us rich.
The Big Idea
In a fallen world, poverty — of pocket and of soul — is the default drift of entropy. It is reversed not by riches but by intention: supremely by the intentional love of God in Christ, who became poor to make many rich.
The Frame — What "Entropy" Means Here
The horizon behind the whole message
Everything Left Alone Runs Down

The second law of thermodynamics says that a closed system always drifts toward disorder — heat spreads out, order decays, things fall apart unless energy is intentionally poured in. Your coffee cools. Your house dusts over. A garden left alone grows weeds, never vegetables. Nobody has to try to make a mess; mess is the default.

Scripture saw the pattern long before physics named it. After the Fall, the ground itself resists us: "thorns and thistles it shall bring forth… by the sweat of your face you shall eat" (Gen 3:18–19). Decay — entropy — is a signature of the curse: bodies age, relationships cool, nations decline, and wealth dissipates. Left to itself, the world runs down, and the name of that running-down, at street level, is poverty.

But here is the gospel seed hidden in the science: the universe is not actually a closed system. God pours energy in. He created order out of chaos, He redeems, He raises the dead. And He calls His people to be agents of that same counter-drift — pushing back the entropy of a fallen world by intention.

Introduction

A woman breaks a jar of costly perfume over Jesus' head. The disciples are indignant: "Why this waste? It could have been sold… and given to the poor" (Matt 26:8–9). It sounds so responsible. And Jesus answers with a line we have quoted ever since — sometimes to excuse ourselves: "For you always have the poor with you, but you will not always have me" (26:11).

We hear it as a shrug — poverty is permanent, so don't bother. But Jesus is quoting Deuteronomy 15, and He means the exact opposite. He is naming the entropy of a fallen world — and telling us how to live inside it.

Three things about living against the drift.

The Message
I
The Drift Is Real — Poverty Is the Default
Matthew 26:11 · Deuteronomy 15:11 · Genesis 3:17–19
A.Jesus states it plainly: "you always have the poor with you." He is not being cynical; He is being honest about a world under the curse. Poverty is not an occasional glitch — it is the gravity of a fallen world.
B.He is quoting Moses: "there will never cease to be poor in the land" (Deut 15:11). The drift is built in. Eden became thorns; the ground now yields weeds by default and bread only "by the sweat of your face."
C.This kills two lies at once: the utopian lie that the right system will finally abolish poverty on its own, and the fatalist lie that nothing can be done. The truth is soberer and more hopeful: the drift is real, and it must be actively resisted.
Illustration
Leave a garden for one summer. You will not return to more tomatoes — you will return to weeds, every time. You never have to plan the weeds; they are the default. Order is the thing that costs effort. So it is with money, marriages, bodies, churches, and souls.
If the drift is toward poverty, surely the cure is riches? That is exactly the disciples' assumption — and it is wrong.
II
Riches Are Not the Cure — Intention Is
Deuteronomy 15:11 · Luke 15:11–16 · Proverbs 6:6–11
A.The disciples think money is the answer: sell it, give it. But poverty's true opposite is not wealth — it is intention. Wealth with no intention simply dissipates: the prodigal took real riches and "squandered" them into a pig-pen (Luke 15:13–16).
B.That is why Deuteronomy 15:11 does not say "get rich." It says, "you shall open wide your hand." The command against poverty is an intentional, repeated act — open hands, not full accounts. The permanence of the poor is the very reason to keep giving, not to stop.
C.The same law governs the soul. A marriage, a friendship, a walk with God does not stay rich by having once been rich; un-tended, it drifts to poverty no matter how gifted you are. Grace received but not cultivated grows cold. Intention is the only thing that ever pushes back the drift.
Illustration
Give two people the same windfall. A year later the difference between them will not be the size of the gift — it will be intention: one budgeted, invested, gave; the other drifted. Same riches; opposite outcomes. Wealth was never the variable. Intention was.
But here is the terror: no amount of intention of ours can finally beat the drift. We need Someone from outside the system.
III
The Deliverer Reverses the Drift — Grace from Outside
2 Corinthians 8:9 · 1 Corinthians 15:42 · Mark 14:8–9
A.Left to entropy, every one of us is headed to the ultimate poverty — the grave, the final running-down. No riches reverse that. The system is closed and cooling… unless energy comes from outside it.
B.So God stepped in — intentionally: "though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor, so that you by his poverty might become rich" (2 Cor 8:9). Christ deliberately entered our poverty to hand us His wealth. That is the most intentional act in history.
C.And the resurrection is the reversal of entropy itself: "sown in corruption… raised in incorruption" (1 Cor 15:42). In Christ the drift runs backward — decay gives way to new creation. Our intentional generosity now is not grim duty; it is joining God's counter-drift.
Illustration
The disciples looked at the shattered perfume jar and saw entropy — value dissipated, wasted. Jesus looked at the same act and saw the one truly imperishable thing in the room: "She has done a beautiful thing… wherever the gospel is preached… what she has done will be told" (Mark 14:8–9). Poured out on Christ, her "waste" is the only thing there that will never decay.
So the Lord turns and asks: where is entropy quietly winning in your life — because you have left it un-intended?
Guard Against the Misreading
"The poor you will always have with you" has been misused to excuse neglect — it's hopeless, so why give? But Jesus quotes Deuteronomy 15 precisely to command the opposite: "there will never cease to be poor… therefore open wide your hand" (15:11). The permanence of the need is the reason for permanent generosity. He is defending one beautiful, intentional act of devotion — not dismissing the poor He came to lift.
Application
Where Is the Drift Winning?
Name the place you've been coasting — and choose one intentional act against the entropy.
1.Name it. Where has "default" become decay because you stopped intending — your finances, your marriage, your body, your devotional life, the neighbour at your gate? Entropy doesn't need your permission; it just needs your passivity.
2.Open your hand now — don't wait to be rich. Generosity is an act of intention, not a level of income. Pick one concrete, repeatable open-handed act this week (a gift, a meal, a debt forgiven, time given).
3.Cultivate, don't coast. Choose one intentional rhythm to re-tend a "garden" you've let go — a weekly date, a fixed time in the Word, a hard conversation. Order costs effort; schedule the effort.
4.Pour it out on Christ. The one investment that never decays is what is poured out in devotion to Jesus. Give Him your most extravagant, intentional "waste."
Conclusion
The Landing

The universe is running down. Your body is running down. Your money, left alone, runs down; so does your marriage, and so does your soul. And no amount of riches can stop the drift — that is what "you will always have the poor with you" finally means. Entropy always wins… in a closed system.

But the good news is that the system is not closed. "Though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor" — God stepped in from outside, intentionally, into our poverty and our grave, and on the third day the drift ran backward. Death itself was sown in corruption and raised in incorruption.

"You will not always have me," Jesus said — but you did. He came. He became poor. He rose. So do not drift. Live intentionally in the light of the One who reversed the drift — and pour out the perfume.
