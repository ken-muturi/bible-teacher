Sermon Outline
Bold Obedience
Stepping in before the river parts
Preaching Outline · 3 points
Primary Text
Joshua 3:7–17
Crossing the Jordan
Supporting
Daniel 3:16–18 · Esther 4:16 · Acts 4:13, 29–31 · Heb 11:8
Aim
To move hearers to obey the command they already know but have delayed — trusting God for the outcome.
The Big Idea
Bold obedience does what God says before it sees how it will work — because it trusts the One who said it. God parts the river as we step in, not before.
Introduction

We love the miracle of the parted Jordan. What we forget is that the miracle came after wet feet. God told the priests carrying the ark to march straight into a river at flood stage (Josh 3:15). The water did not divide when they prayed about it, planned for it, or watched it from the bank. It divided "as soon as… the feet of the priests… were dipped in the brink of the water" (3:15–16). The command was: step in — then you'll see it move.

That is the exact opposite of how we prefer to obey. We want to see the sea split, the money arrive, the door open, the fear lift — and then we'll obey. But God very often reverses the order: the way opens as we obey, not before. Faith that waits for the water to part before it moves will stand on the bank forever.

This morning: three marks of the obedience that gets its feet wet.

The Message
I
Bold Obedience Obeys the Word, Not the Odds
Joshua 3:13 · Hebrews 11:8
A.The command came before the path was visible. "The waters… shall stand in a heap" — future tense, promised but not yet seen. The priests had only a word to go on, and a word from God was enough.
B.Abraham "obeyed… and he went out, not knowing where he was going" (Heb 11:8). Bold obedience does not require a map; it requires a Voice.
C.Faith's arithmetic: one clear command from God outweighs a thousand visible obstacles. The question is never "Can I see how?" but "Has God said?"
Illustration
A river at flood stage is the worst possible moment to cross — and that is precisely when God said go. He often lets the obstacle swell to its highest just before He asks you to step, so that when it parts, no one doubts who did it.
But a word like that will cost you something to obey — which is the second mark.
II
Bold Obedience Counts the Cost and Steps Anyway
Daniel 3:16–18 · Esther 4:16
A.Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego: "Our God… is able to deliver us… But if not, be it known to you, O king, that we will not serve your gods" (3:17–18). Their obedience was not contingent on rescue.
B.Esther walked into the throne room uninvited on pain of death: "If I perish, I perish" (4:16). Bold obedience will risk what it cannot afford to lose.
C.Boldness is not the absence of fear; it is fear overruled by a higher allegiance — "we must obey God rather than men" (Acts 5:29).
Illustration
"But if not" is the hinge of real faith. Anyone will obey a God who guarantees the furnace won't burn. The three Hebrews obeyed a God who might let them burn — and trusted Him past the flames. That is the courage that cannot be bribed or threatened.
So where does courage like that come from? Never from us — which is the third mark.
III
Bold Obedience Draws Its Courage from God's Presence, Not Our Strength
Joshua 1:9 · Acts 4:13, 29–31
A.The command to be brave rests on a promise of presence: "Be strong and courageous… for the LORD your God is with you wherever you go" (Josh 1:9). Take away the presence and the command is cruel; with it, it is fuel.
B.Threatened by the council, the apostles did not pray for safety — they prayed for boldness: "grant to your servants to continue to speak your word with all boldness" (Acts 4:29). And the place was shaken and they were filled.
C.They were "uneducated, common men," yet the rulers marvelled and "recognized that they had been with Jesus" (4:13). Boldness is the overflow of His presence, not the product of our personality.
Illustration
Notice what the early church asked for under pressure. Not, "Lord, remove the threat." But, "Lord, make us bold in it." When we stop praying for the water to disappear and start praying for courage to step in, heaven starts to move.
So the Lord turns and asks: where is your Jordan?
Application
Where Is Your Jordan?
The command you already know, but have delayed because you can't yet see how it works out.
1.Name it. The conversation you've avoided. The confession overdue. The giving that feels reckless. The call you've argued with. The person you must forgive. The witness you keep swallowing. What has God already said that you have not yet done?
2.Step in. Don't wait for the water to part first. Obey the next step in front of you, and leave the outcome with the God who commanded it. Wet feet come before dry ground.
3.Pray "but if not" faith. Resolve to obey even if it costs, even if God doesn't rescue you the way you'd script it. Obedience that only follows a guaranteed outcome isn't yet trust.
4.Ask for boldness, not just relief. Like the church in Acts 4, pray less "take this away" and more "make me brave in it — you are with me."
Conclusion
The Landing

The water stood at flood stage until the feet got wet. Hear this carefully: God is not standing back, waiting to part your sea so that you'll finally obey. He is waiting for your obedience to be the moment the sea parts. The miracle is on the far side of the first wet step.

And we can step boldly only because Someone stepped first. Jesus was "obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross" (Phil 2:8) — He walked into the flood of judgment that should have swept us away, and He did not turn back. He got no "but if not"; the furnace fell full on Him, so that it never will on us.

Our bold obedience is never the price of His love — it is the answer to it. He got His feet wet first. Now He says to you: step in.
