The Frame — What "Entropy" Means
The horizon behind the series
Everything Left Alone Runs Down

The second law of thermodynamics: a closed system always drifts toward disorder unless energy is intentionally poured in. Coffee cools; a house dusts over; a garden left alone grows weeds, never vegetables. Nobody has to try to make a mess — mess is the default.

Scripture saw it first. After the Fall the ground itself resists us — "thorns and thistles… by the sweat of your face" (Gen 3:18–19). Decay is a signature of the curse, and its name at street level is poverty. Over three sessions we'll trace the drift — and the God who reverses it. Today: the drift is real, and generosity is how we push back.

Introduction

A woman breaks a jar of costly perfume over Jesus. The disciples are indignant: "Why this waste? It could have been sold… and given to the poor" (Matt 26:8–9). Jesus answers with a line we've quoted ever since — sometimes to excuse ourselves: "you always have the poor with you" (26:11).

We hear a shrug — poverty is permanent, so why bother? But Jesus is quoting Deuteronomy 15, and He means the opposite.

Two things about living against the drift.

The Message
I
The Drift Is Real — Poverty Is the Default
Matthew 26:11 · Deuteronomy 15:11 · Genesis 3:17–19
A."You always have the poor with you" is not cynicism; it is honesty about a world under the curse. Poverty is not an occasional glitch — it is the gravity of a fallen world.
B.Jesus quotes Moses: "there will never cease to be poor in the land" (Deut 15:11). The drift is built in — Eden became thorns; the ground yields weeds by default and bread only "by the sweat of your face."
C.This kills two lies at once: the utopian lie that the right system will abolish poverty on its own, and the fatalist lie that nothing can be done. The truth is soberer and more hopeful: the drift is real, and it must be actively resisted.
Illustration
Leave a garden for one summer and you will not return to more tomatoes — you will return to weeds, every time. You never have to plan the weeds; they are the default. Order is the thing that costs effort — with gardens, money, bodies, and souls alike.
If the drift is toward poverty, surely the cure is riches? That is the disciples' assumption — and it is wrong.
II
Riches Are Not the Cure — Intention Is
Deuteronomy 15:11 · Luke 15:11–16
A.The disciples think money is the answer: sell it, give it. But poverty's true opposite is not wealth — it is intention. Wealth with no intention simply dissipates: the prodigal took real riches and "squandered" them into a pig-pen (Luke 15:13–16).
B.So Deuteronomy 15:11 does not say "get rich." It says, "you shall open wide your hand." The command against poverty is an intentional, repeated act — open hands, not full accounts. The permanence of the poor is the reason to keep giving, not to stop.
C.Generosity is therefore a matter of posture, not income. You do not wait until you are rich to open your hand; you open it now, on purpose, again and again.
Illustration
Give two people the same windfall. A year later the difference between them will not be the size of the gift — it will be intention: one budgeted, invested, gave; the other drifted. Same riches; opposite outcomes. Wealth was never the variable.
So the Lord turns and asks: where is entropy quietly winning because you have left it un-intended?
Guard Both Edges
"The poor you always have" has been misused to excuse neglect — but Jesus quotes Deut 15 to command the opposite: "therefore open wide your hand." And guard the other edge: "poverty is a failure of intention" describes the drift of a fallen world, never a verdict on the person in need — much poverty is injustice, calamity, or oppression, not fault. The intention this text summons is ours ("open your hand"), not a diagnosis of why the poor are poor.
Application
Open Your Hand — Now
Generosity is an act of intention, not a level of income.
1.Name the drift. Where has "default" become decay because you stopped intending — your finances, your stewardship, the neighbour at your gate?
2.Pick one open-handed act this week — a gift, a meal, a debt forgiven, time given. Repeatable beats grand.
3.Steward, don't squander. Put one intentional guardrail on what you already have (a budget line, a giving plan) so it isn't quietly drifting away.
Conclusion
The Landing

The world runs down; money left alone runs down; and no amount of riches finally stops the drift — that is what "you always have the poor with you" means. Entropy always wins in a closed system.

But we are not left to shrug. The permanence of the need is God's call to permanent, intentional, open-handed love. So do not wait to be rich — open your hand today.

Next time: there is a subtler poverty still — the kind you can feel while your hands are full. We'll meet a camp that was rich in glory and couldn't see it.
