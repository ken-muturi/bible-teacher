Sermon Outline
Drawn Into the Dance
Communion with the Holy Spirit — brought home into the life of the Trinity
Preaching Outline · 3 points
Simple one-page version Companion · A Person, Not a Power
Text & Frame
Ephesians 2:18; John 16:12–15, framed by 2 Corinthians 13:14 (the fellowship of the Spirit)
Supporting
Rom 8:15–16 · Rom 5:5 · Gal 4:6 · John 14:23 · John 17:21–23
Aim
To move hearers from feeling like outsiders to God into the settled joy of belonging inside the Father's and Son's shared love.
The Big Idea
The Spirit's gift — koinōnia — is never to point to himself, but to usher you inside the shared life and love of the Father and the Son, until you cry "Abba" and God's own love floods your heart. Communion with the Spirit is how you come home into the life of God.
The Frame — Three Persons in One Short Verse
The Spirit never travels alone
To the Father, Through the Son, By the Spirit

"Through him [Christ] we both have access to the Father by one Spirit" (Eph 2:18). In thirteen words, the whole Trinity: we come to the Father, through the Son, by the Spirit. The Spirit is not a separate destination — he is the one who brings you in.

That is his very character. "He will glorify me," Jesus said (John 16:14) — the Spirit floods the light onto the Son and leads you to the Father. So communion with the Spirit is never the Spirit instead of the Father and the Son. It is the Spirit taking you by the hand and walking you inside the family.

Paul's last word to Corinth seals it: "the grace of the Lord Jesus… the love of God… and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit" (2 Cor 13:14). The Spirit's assigned gift is fellowship — shared life with the triune God.

Introduction

Many believers live in God's house like a hired servant, not a son. They keep the rules, do the chores, and stand just outside the warmth of the family room, half-sure they belong. They know about the Father's love the way you know a fact — but they've never felt the door open and a voice say, "you're family; come in."

That opening of the door is the Spirit's special work. He was sent, precisely, to bring outsiders home — to take people who felt like servants and teach their hearts to say "Abba."

Three ways the Spirit draws you into the life of God.

The Message
I
He Brings You to the Father — The Cry "Abba"
Romans 8:15–16 · Ephesians 2:18 · Galatians 4:6
A."The Spirit you received brought about your adoption to sonship. And by him we cry, 'Abba, Father'" (Rom 8:15). The Spirit does not leave you at the gate — he gives you the family word and puts it in your mouth.
B."Abba" is intimate — the word a child uses at home. God "sent the Spirit of his Son into our hearts, the Spirit who calls out, 'Abba, Father'" (Gal 4:6). The very relationship the Son has with the Father, the Spirit opens to you.
C.So you have access — welcome, a standing invitation — to the Father (Eph 2:18). Not an audience you must book; a home you may enter. The Spirit is the one who walks you in.
Illustration
An adopted child's first "Daddy" is a landmark moment — the day a legal fact becomes a felt relationship. The papers were already signed; now the heart has caught up. The Spirit is the one who moves "Father" from the paperwork of theology to the first warm cry of the heart.
But how do we know a stirring is really the Spirit, and not just emotion? By where it points.
II
He Glorifies the Son — Always Toward Jesus
John 16:13–15
A."He will glorify me, for it is from me that he will receive what he will make known" (16:14). The Spirit is gloriously self-effacing — his joy is to make the Son beautiful to you, not to draw a crowd to himself.
B.This gives us a sure test. A true work of the Spirit always leaves you loving, trusting, treasuring Christ more. If an experience inflates the spectacular but leaves Jesus smaller, it is not the Spirit's doing.
C.And through this Christ-ward work, you are brought into the Son's own place: united to Jesus, you share his sonship, his access, his welcome. The Spirit joins you to the Son so the Father's "beloved" covers you too.
Illustration
Think of a friend who only ever talks about your good qualities to others — never angling for attention, always lifting someone else. You trust a friend like that completely. The Spirit is forever talking up the Son. His humility is our assurance that he can be trusted.
He brings us to the Father, he exalts the Son — and then he does the most tender thing of all.
III
He Pours In the Love — The Father's Heart Made Real
Romans 5:5 · Romans 8:16
A."God's love has been poured out into our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us" (Rom 5:5). Not sprinkled — poured, lavishly. The Spirit's ministry is to make the Father's love an experienced reality, not a cold doctrine.
B.He "testifies with our spirit that we are God's children" (Rom 8:16) — an inner witness that steadies the soul when our own heart is unsure. Assurance of the Father's love is his gift, not our achievement.
C.This is the summit of communion: not merely to know God loves you, but to be brought so far inside that love that it becomes your joy, your home, the settled ground you stand on. The Spirit carries you into the warmth of the family room.
Illustration
There is a difference between reading a letter that says "you are loved" and feeling arms actually close around you. The Spirit does not replace the letter of Scripture — he presses its truth so deep that the cold page becomes a felt embrace. "God loves me" stops being information and becomes experience.
If this is the Spirit's work, two mistakes will rob us of it.
Guard Against Two Errors
First, a Spirit-centred spirituality that eclipses Christ — making the Spirit and his manifestations the main attraction. The Spirit himself refuses this; he glorifies the Son (John 16:14), so any "move of the Spirit" that sidelines Jesus has misnamed itself. Second, a cold, diagram-only Trinitarianism — believers who can define the three Persons but have never cried "Abba," who stand in the hallway of correct doctrine and never enter the family room. The goal is neither spectacle nor a theory on a page: it is to be brought in — to come to the Father, through the Son, by the Spirit, and to know in your bones that you belong.
A Further Dimension — Voices from the Teachers
John Owen
Of Communion with God the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost
Taught that believers hold distinct communion with each Person of the Trinity — and that the Spirit's peculiar work is to be our Comforter, bringing home to the heart the Father's love and the Son's grace. Communion with the Spirit is consolation. [VERIFY: wording.]
Sinclair Ferguson
The Holy Spirit (Contours of Christian Theology)
Shows the Spirit's ministry is thoroughly Christ-centred and Trinitarian: by uniting us to Christ, the Spirit brings us to share in the Son's own relationship to the Father. The Spirit never works in isolation from the Son he glorifies. [VERIFY: wording.]
Tim Keller · John Piper · J.I. Packer
on being brought inside the love
Keller pictured the Trinity as a dance of self-giving love we are drawn into; Piper presses that the Spirit pours in the Father's love so that God himself becomes our joy (Rom 5:5); and Packer (Knowing God) calls adoption — the Spirit teaching us to say "Father" — the highest privilege the gospel gives. Application
Come Inside — You Belong There
Live as a child in the family room, not a servant at the door.
1.Pray the Trinity on purpose. Begin a prayer coming consciously to the Father, through Jesus, by the Spirit. Say "Abba" — and believe you're welcome.
2.Measure by Christ. Test every spiritual experience by one question: did it make Jesus bigger and dearer? Keep what passes; set aside what doesn't.
3.Ask the love to land. Pray Romans 5:5: "Spirit, pour the Father's love into my heart — make it real, not just true."
4.Rest in belonging. Stop living like an outsider earning entry. In the Spirit you are already home. Act, pray, and rest from that settled place.
Conclusion
The Landing

You may have walked in feeling like a servant in God's house — dutiful, correct, and somehow still outside. Hear what the Spirit came to do: not to keep you busy in the yard, but to open the door and walk you inside the family.

He brings you to the Father. He makes the Son beautiful to you. He pours the Father's love into your heart until "Abba" rises from somewhere deep and true.

This is communion with the Holy Spirit: not the Spirit instead of God, but the Spirit bringing you home into God. The door is open. You belong inside. Come in.
