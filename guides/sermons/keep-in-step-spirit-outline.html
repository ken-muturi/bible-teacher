Sermon Outline
Keep in Step
Communion with the Holy Spirit — one day, one step at a time
Preaching Outline · 3 points
Simple one-page version Companion · A Person, Not a Power
Text & Frame
Galatians 5:16–26, framed by Ephesians 5:18 ("go on being filled with the Spirit")
Supporting
Eph 4:30 · 1 Thess 5:19 · John 14:21, 23 · Rom 8:14 · Psalm 1:2–3
Aim
To move hearers from living off a past experience to a daily, responsive walk that keeps the Spirit's pace.
The Big Idea
Communion with the Spirit is not a mountaintop you remember but a pace you keep. You cannot live on yesterday's fullness. Keep in step with him — today — by quick obedience, a guarded fellowship, and a daily return to be filled again.
The Frame — The Picture Paul Paints
Two walking words
Walk By — and Keep in Step With

Paul gives us two verbs for life with the Spirit. "Walk by the Spirit" (5:16, peripateō) — the ordinary word for daily conduct, a walking pace, not a sprint. And "keep in step with the Spirit" (5:25, stoicheō) — a military word for marching in line, in rank, in time with the others.

Both pictures say the same thing: communion is a cadence, not a peak. You don't bottle the Spirit's nearness on a good night and live off it for months. You match his stride again this morning — not running ahead of him in the flesh, not dragging behind him in disobedience, but moving when he moves.

So the question is not "when did I last feel close?" but "am I keeping his pace today?"

Introduction

There is a kind of believer who lives entirely in the past tense — "at that camp, in that season, God was so real to me." It's a beautiful memory and a quiet tragedy, because the Spirit is not a souvenir. He is a companion for the walk you are on right now.

Israel learned this with manna: they could not hoard it; yesterday's gathering bred worms by morning (Exod 16:20). They had to go out and gather fresh, daily. Communion with the Spirit works the same way.

Three things it takes to keep the Spirit's pace, day by day.

The Message
I
Walk at His Pace — Not Ahead, Not Behind
Galatians 5:16, 25
A."Walk by the Spirit, and you will not gratify the desires of the flesh" (5:16). The walk is the weapon: keeping step with him is how the flesh is starved. Not gritted-teeth willpower, but following Someone.
B.We break stride two ways. We run ahead — acting in our own strength, our own timing, our own plans, then asking him to bless the sprint. Or we lag behind — dawdling in disobedience while he has already moved on.
C.Keeping in step means a moment-by-moment surrender: "Spirit, your pace, not mine." It is ordinary and continual — the walking pace of a whole life, checked and re-set through the day.
Illustration
Anyone who has walked hand in hand with a small child knows the discipline of pace: shorten your stride, or you drag them; match them, and the walk is sweet. We are the ones who must adjust — the Spirit does not sprint to keep up with our ambition, nor dawdle to excuse our sloth. We keep his step.
But a walk can be interrupted. Two things, especially, break the fellowship of the road.
II
Guard the Fellowship — Don't Grieve, Don't Quench
Ephesians 4:30 · 1 Thessalonians 5:19
A.Don't grieve him (Eph 4:30). Grief is relational pain — caused by bitterness, harsh words, the sins named in the verses around it (4:31). Sin doesn't just break rules; it wounds the Friend walking beside you.
B.Don't quench him (1 Thess 5:19). To quench is to douse a fire — to smother his promptings, ignore his nudges, say "not now" until you stop hearing them. Grieving is about sin; quenching is about suppression.
C.The remedy for both is quick, glad responsiveness: when he convicts, repent without delay; when he prompts, obey while the nudge is warm. Sensitivity is kept by use and lost by neglect.
Illustration
A campfire ignored goes out — not with a bang, but by slow neglect, one un-tended ember at a time. No single moment killed it; a hundred small "not nows" did. So communion is rarely lost in a blowout. It cools by a thousand quenched nudges and un-repented griefs.
Walking and guarding — but where does the strength to keep going come from?
III
Keep Being Filled — Come Back for More
Ephesians 5:18–19
A."Be filled with the Spirit" (5:18) is present-tense and continuous — go on being filled. It is not a one-time tank you top up at conversion; it is a daily, repeatable filling.
B.And it has ordinary means: the very next verses point to the Word dwelling in us, singing, thankfulness, and the fellowship of the saints (5:19–21; cf. Col 3:16). Communion is cultivated at the ordinary wells — Scripture, prayer, worship, and one another.
C.So the rhythm is: gather fresh. Come back each day to be filled again. The fruit of the Spirit (5:22–23) grows on the branch that stays connected — not on the one that remembers once being green.
Illustration
A sail doesn't generate wind; it catches it. The sailor's whole skill is to keep the sail raised and set so that when the wind blows, the boat moves. The means of grace are how we keep our sails up. We cannot manufacture the Spirit's filling — but we can be found in the places the wind tends to blow.
Before we apply it, guard against two ways of getting "keeping in step" wrong.
Guard Against Two Errors
First, legalism — turning "keep in step" into fleshly striving, a checklist you grind out in your own strength. That is the very thing Galatians fights; the walk is by the Spirit, powered by him, not by willpower. Second, passivity — a "let go and let God" that waits limp for a feeling and calls it rest. The verbs are active: walk, keep in step, be filled. And beware chasing the sensation of fullness as proof — the fruit of the Spirit (love, joy, peace, patience) is the truer evidence than any goosebump. Spirit-empowered, wholehearted following — that is the narrow road between the ditches.
A Further Dimension — Voices from the Teachers
J.I. Packer
Keep in Step with the Spirit
Took this very verse for his title and theme: the Spirit-filled life is a steady, daily matching of the Spirit's stride — holiness as walking with him — not a series of dramatic peaks. He also pictured the Spirit's work as a floodlight that keeps throwing our attention onto Christ. [VERIFY: wording.]
R.T. Kendall
The Sensitivity of the Spirit
Taught that the Spirit is like a dove — gentle and easily grieved — and that the secret of ongoing communion is learning his ways so as not to grieve or quench him. Quick obedience keeps the fellowship, and the sense of his presence, sweet. [VERIFY: wording.]
Jack Hayford & A.W. Tozer
on cultivating the presence
Hayford modelled cultivating the Spirit's presence through reverent, wholehearted worship; Tozer called the Christian life a lifelong pursuit of God, refusing to settle for yesterday's nearness. Both insist the daily return to God is not duty but desire. [VERIFY: wording.]
Application
Keep the Pace This Week
Small, daily, repeatable — the cadence of a real walk.
1.Set the pace each morning. Before the day bolts, surrender it: "Spirit, your pace today — lead, and I'll follow."
2.Obey the nudge quickly. When he prompts and it agrees with Scripture, act while it's warm. Delay is how we quench.
3.Clear what grieves him. Name and forsake one bitterness or habit dampening the fellowship (Eph 4:31).
4.Come back to be filled. Keep a daily appointment at the wells — a Psalm, honest prayer, a song — and ask to be filled afresh.
Conclusion
The Landing

You cannot live the Christian life on stored manna. The night you remember was real — thank God for it — but the Spirit did not come to be a memory. He came to walk the whole road with you, at a pace you can keep every single day.

So don't strive, and don't drift. Walk by the Spirit. Keep in step with the Spirit. Say your quick yeses. Guard the fellowship. And come back each morning with your sail up, to be filled again.

Fresh grace for today's steps — that is what he is offering. Gather it fresh.
