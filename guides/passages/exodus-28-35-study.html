Exodus 28–35

Heart of Wisdom: who made the tabernacle, and where did their wisdom come from?

Exodus · Deep study · NIV primary, NKJV compared · The builders were “wise of heart,” and God says he filled them. This panel asks which comes first.

“…and I have filled him with the Spirit of God, with wisdom, with understanding, with knowledge and with all kinds of skills.” Exodus 31:3 (NIV), said of Bezalel. Read chs. 28–35 from your own Bible; only key verses are quoted here.

The question, answered

Short answer: the text points mainly one way. They were wise-hearted because God filled and gave, not filled because they had earned it. But “wise-hearted” also names a real group of skilled people God worked through, and their willing hearts were part of the story.

1. God is the one who gives. God is the subject of the verbs in 28:3 (“whom I have filled”), 31:3, 31:6, 35:31, 35:35 and 36:1–2 (“to whom the Lord has given”). No verse says God filled them in response to their skill.

2. Calling comes before the description. In 31:2 God says “I have chosen Bezalel” by name, then fills him. Nothing in the text offers wisdom as the qualification.

3. But “wise of heart” was already a category. The phrase (chakmei lev) appears as a description of artisans (28:3; 35:10, 25; 36:1–2). Israel had skilled workers, and 31:6 can be read as God adding wisdom to the wise-hearted. So there are two layers: ordinary skill, which is also God’s gift (a creation gift, compare Isa 28:24–29), and a special filling for this sacred task.

4. The human side is a willing heart. Hearts are “stirred” and “moved” (35:21, 26, 29; 36:2). That is participation and consent, never the cause of the gift.

So: God chooses and fills; the gift lifts ordinary skill into sacred work and adds the ability to teach (35:34); willing hearts say yes. It’s a gift received, not a prize won. [VERIFY: Hebrew of 31:6 is read both ways; see pressure points.]

Word studies

Wise of heart · 28:3; 31:6; 35:10, 25; 36:1–2

chakam lev, chokmah (חֲכַם־לֵב)

Chokmah can mean craft expertise (Isa 40:20) and moral wisdom (Proverbs). “Heart” is the seat of mind and will. NIV: “skilled workers”; NKJV: “gifted artisans.” Lost in English: “skilled” hides that the same word is Proverbs’ wisdom, so skill and wisdom aren’t separate categories.

Fill · 28:3; 31:3; 35:31, 35

male’ (מָלֵא)

The same verb describes the Lord’s glory filling the tabernacle (40:34–35) [VERIFY]. God fills the builders, then fills the building. Lost: the link between the two fillings.

Spirit of God · 31:3; 35:31

ruach Elohim (רוּחַ אֱלֹהִים)

In the OT, the Spirit typically comes on people for specific tasks (e.g., Judges 3:10; Num 11:25) [VERIFY]. Here the task is craft, which is unusual. NIV and NKJV both say “Spirit of God.”

Wisdom, understanding, knowledge · 31:3; 35:31

chokmah, tevunah, da’at (חָכְמָה תְבוּנָה דַּעַת)

The same triad appears in Prov 3:19–20, where God made creation by wisdom, understanding, and knowledge [VERIFY]. Many read Bezalel as a small-scale echo of the Creator. NKJV: “workmanship” for the final term (melakhah), which is also the word for work in the Sabbath law (31:14).

Stirred heart · 35:21, 26, 29; 36:2

nasa’ lev, nadiv (נָשָׂא לֵב)

Literally “lifted up” the heart (and “willing,” nadiv). NIV “moved”; NKJV “stirred.” Lost: the picture of the heart being lifted, which is something done to the person and yet freely joined in.

Translation note · 28:3 and 31:6

NIV and NKJV differ in how they render “wise of heart” and “give/put wisdom.” Compare both editions’ wording in 28:3 and 31:6 [VERIFY]; the choice of “skilled” versus “gifted” affects how the cause comes across.

Historical and cultural context

Setting. The tabernacle account sits at Sinai, dated around 1446 BC on the early view of the Exodus or the 13th century BC on the late view [VERIFY]. Israel had worked in Egypt’s building trades (Ex 1:14) and left with gold and jewelry (12:35–36), so both skills and materials were at hand.

Temple building in the ancient world. In the ancient Near East, temple construction was a royal and divine project, and craftsmen were sometimes linked to patron deities [VERIFY: e.g., the Ugaritic craftsman god Kothar-wa-Khasis]. Exodus makes a sharp claim: the Lord himself gifts his builders.

Tribes. Bezalel is from Judah, a leading tribe, and Oholiab from Dan (31:2, 6), often noted as a less prominent tribe [VERIFY]. God partners across ranks.

Illustrations

Everyday · the violin

A child may have a good ear naturally, but a master’s teaching takes the playing somewhere the child couldn’t reach alone. Natural gift and the master’s work are both real, and they’re not rivals.

Non-religious · apprenticeship

A master carpenter picks an apprentice and trains them until they can train others. Bezalel and Oholiab get exactly that: skill, plus “ability to teach” (35:34).

History · Bach

Bach is reported to have marked many manuscripts with Soli Deo Gloria, treating composition as offering [VERIFY]. It’s craft done as worship, and the opposite of a golden calf.

Interpretive pressure points

1. The main debate: how to read 31:6

The Hebrew puts wisdom “in the heart of all the wise-hearted.” Some read this as God adding wisdom to people who already have skill; others as God creating the wise-hearted by giving wisdom. Both fit the verse, so don’t build a theology on this verse alone. Chs. 28:3 and 31:3 carry the weight.

2. The most common misread: “spiritual gifts” versus “natural talents”

People often split ability into sacred (Spirit-given) and secular (natural). Exodus doesn’t make that split. The same word, wisdom, covers a weaver’s skill and a priest’s role, and God claims both as his gift.

3. Open question: is this an ongoing indwelling?

OT filling is usually task-specific (Bezalel for the tabernacle). How that compares to the New Covenant’s indwelling Spirit (Joel 2:28; John 14:17; Acts 2) is a legitimate teaching question and best left open for discussion.

Tradition note Pentecostal and charismatic readers often cite Bezalel for Spirit-empowered creativity; Reformed readers often add common grace. [VERIFY: which framing fits your context]
