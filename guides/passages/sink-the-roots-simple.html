Sink the Roots
The Tree Planted by Water — a simple teaching

The Text
"Blessed is the man who trusts in the LORD… He is like a tree planted by water, that sends out its roots by the stream, and does not fear when heat comes… it does not cease to bear fruit."
Jeremiah 17:7–8 (ESV)
The Big Idea (in plain words)
The Bible pictures a good life as a tree planted by a stream. When the heat and drought come — and they always come — a shrub in the desert shrivels, but the tree by water stays green and keeps bearing fruit.

Why? Not because the tree escapes the drought, but because its roots reach the water. And the Bible is clear about what that water is: God himself, known through his word — delighted in, thought about, trusted, obeyed, and lived in.

Last time we pulled the thorns that choke the word. This time we do the other half: drive the roots down deep, until the word holds you.
How to Sink the Roots
1 · Delight in it
Love the word, don't just read it
Roots grow toward what the heart treasures. Learn to enjoy God's word, not just do a duty.
Psalm 1:2
2 · Meditate on it
Chew it over, slowly
Not skimming but pondering — turning a verse over in your mind and prayer until it soaks in.
Psalm 1:2 · Joshua 1:8
3 · Feed & trust & abide
Live on it, lean on it, stay in Christ
The word is daily bread (Deut 8:3); trust and obey it (Jer 17:7); and stay joined to Jesus, the vine (John 15:5).
Deut 8:3 · John 15
For You This Week
Get by the stream. Set a daily time in God's word — the same place your roots can drink every day.
Meditate, don't just read. Take one verse and chew on it all day — repeat it, pray it, ask how it applies.
Memorise one verse. Hidden in the heart, it's a root that keeps feeding you when the drought comes.
Be planted in his house. Roots go deep in the soil of a worshipping community, not alone (Ps 92:13).
