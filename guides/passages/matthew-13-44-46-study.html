The Two Parables
"The kingdom of heaven is like treasure hidden in a field. When a man found it, he hid it again, and then in his joy went and sold all he had and bought that field.

Again, the kingdom of heaven is like a merchant looking for fine pearls. When he found one of great value, he went away and sold everything he had and bought it."
Matthew 13:44–46 (NIV)
The Two Parables Side by Side
Parable 1 · v.44
Treasure in the Field
A man stumbles on buried treasure by accident. Overjoyed, he sells everything to buy the whole field.
Parable 2 · vv.45–46
The Pearl of Great Price
A merchant is deliberately searching for fine pearls. Finding one supreme pearl, he sells everything to buy it.
Same shape — a discovery, a total cost, a single prize. The one difference: one finds by surprise, the other by long searching.
Who is the "man"? — Two Readings
Reading A · Popular
The man is YOU — the believer
The kingdom is so valuable that when you truly see it, you gladly give up everything to have it. A call to wholehearted discipleship (cf. Phil 3:7–8).

Weakness: you cannot buy the kingdom — salvation is free (Isa 55:1; Eph 2:8–9). And Scripture says no one naturally seeks God (Rom 3:11).
Reading B · Stronger
The man is CHRIST
Christ is the one who found the treasure and, "for the joy set before him" (Heb 12:2), sold all he had (Phil 2:7) to buy the field at the cost of his blood (Acts 20:28; 1 Pet 1:18–19). You are the treasure he bought.

Anchor: Jesus himself says "the field is the world" two verses earlier (Matt 13:38).
The Key Word
ἀγοράζω
agorazō — "to buy"
Used of redemption (vv.44, 46)
The same verb the NT uses for Christ "buying" his people: "you were bought with a price" (1 Cor 6:20); "the church, which he obtained with his own blood" (Acts 20:28). The word tilts the parable toward Christ as the buyer — grace stays intact, because he pays, not us.
Cross-References
Matthew 13:38
"The field is the world." Jesus' own key, given in the same chapter — the strongest reason to read Christ as the buyer of the field.
Hebrews 12:2 · Philippians 2:7
"For the joy set before him… he endured the cross"; he "emptied himself." The joy and the self-emptying of the man who sold all he had.
1 Peter 1:18–19
Redeemed "not with perishable things… but with the precious blood of Christ." The purchase price of the field.
Luke 14:33 · Phil 3:7–8
Where the "give up everything for the kingdom" application really belongs — the cost of discipleship, taught directly.
In a sentence
The kingdom is so valuable a person would give everything for it — and the deeper wonder is that Christ already did, and you were the treasure he bought.
