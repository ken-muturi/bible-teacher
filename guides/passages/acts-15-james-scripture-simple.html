How the Church Decided
Settled by the Word — a simple teaching
Acts 15:13–18 · The Jerusalem Council

The Text
"Brothers, listen to me… The words of the prophets are in agreement with this, as it is written: 'I will rebuild David's fallen tent… that the rest of mankind may seek the Lord, even all the Gentiles who bear my name.'"
Acts 15:13, 15–17 (NIV)
The Big Idea (in plain words)
The early church hit its biggest argument yet: did Gentiles have to become Jews — be circumcised and keep the law — to be saved? Get it wrong and the good news of grace is lost, or the church splits in two.

So the leaders met in Jerusalem. They listened as Peter, Paul, and Barnabas told what God had been doing. Then James stood up and settled it — not by his own authority, not by a vote, not by the loudest voice, but by opening the Scriptures: "the words of the prophets agree with this, as it is written."

The most important decision the early church ever made was settled by the Word.
Three Simple Steps They Took
1 · They listened
Take God's work seriously
Peter told how God gave his Spirit to Gentiles; Paul and Barnabas told of signs among the nations. The church paid honest attention to what God was clearly doing.
Acts 15:7–12
2 · They tested it
Check it against Scripture
James didn't stop at the experience. He asked: does God's word agree with this? He turned to Amos 9 — and found God had planned to gather the nations all along.
Acts 15:15–18
3 · They decided
Let the Word settle it
Grounded in Scripture, James gave the verdict: don't burden the Gentiles — protecting grace, while asking a few things to keep the church united at the table.
Acts 15:19–20
For You This Week
Take God's work seriously. Don't ignore what God is clearly doing around you — pay honest attention to it.
But test it by the Word. When you sense "God is doing something," ask: does Scripture agree? Feelings and stories are not enough on their own.
Let Scripture have the final word. In your next hard decision, don't settle it by the loudest voice or plain convenience — open the Bible first.
Hold truth and unity together. Guard the gospel firmly, and work for peace with people who differ — the council did both.
