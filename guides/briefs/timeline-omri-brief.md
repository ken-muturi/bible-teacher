# Omri: The Forgotten Founder
## Builder of Samaria, Father of the Omride Dynasty

**Era:** Divided Kingdom · ~885–874 BC · 1 Kings 16

---

## Era in Context

**United Kingdom** (tail end, ~930 BC and earlier) → **Divided Kingdom (Israel and Judah)** ~930–722 BC

Timeline window covered: 910 BC to 830 BC

## Figures — Lifespans and Reigns (~910–830 BC)

### Israel (Northern Kingdom)

**Omri** — ~900–874 BC (reigned 885–874 BC)
Founded Samaria. The focus figure of this timeline. 1 Kings 16:21–28.

**Ahab** — ~875–853 BC (reigned 874–853 BC)
Son of Omri. Married Jezebel of Sidon. 1 Kings 16:29.

**Ahaziah of Israel** — reigned 853–852 BC
Son of Ahab. 2 Kings 1.

**Joram of Israel** — reigned 852–841 BC
Son of Ahab. Killed by Jehu. 2 Kings 9.

### Judah (Southern Kingdom)

**Asa of Judah** — ~910–869 BC
Overlapped Omri's entire reign. Sought alliance with Ben-Hadad I against Israel (1 Kings 15:18–20). Known for religious reforms, yet stumbled in later years.

**Jehoshaphat of Judah** — ~872–848 BC
Allied with Ahab. His son Jehoram of Judah married Athaliah — bringing Omride blood into Judah's royal house. The alliance that nearly destroyed the Davidic line.

**Athaliah** — queen regnant 841–835 BC
Omri's granddaughter. Married Jehoram of Judah. Seized the throne of Judah after Jehu's revolution. The only queen regnant in the biblical record. 2 Kings 11.

### Prophets

**Elijah the Tishbite** — ~875–848 BC (active)
Called during Ahab's reign — the direct inheritor of Omri's covenantal failures. Confronted Baal worship (Jezebel's import), declared drought, stood alone at Carmel (1 Kings 17–18). The prophetic antidote to the Omride dynasty.

**Obadiah (the palace official)** — fl. ~874–853 BC
Ahab's palace manager who feared God — hid 100 prophets from Jezebel. A quiet act of courage inside the Omride court. Distinct from Obadiah the prophet. 1 Kings 18:3–4.

**Elisha** — ~852–798 BC (active)
Took over Elijah's mantle. Active through and beyond the Omride era.

### Foreign Powers

**Ethbaal of Sidon** — fl. ~887–856 BC
King of Sidon and Tyre. Omri's ally, sealed by the marriage of Ahab to his daughter Jezebel. The price of geopolitical stability was institutional Baal worship in Israel.

**Ben-Hadad I of Aram-Syria** — ~880–841 BC
Rival of Omri and Ahab. Bribed by Asa of Judah to attack Israel. Later made treaty with Ahab. The ongoing Syrian threat shaped Omride foreign policy.

**Shalmaneser III of Assyria** — ~858–824 BC
Not yet a direct threat during Omri's lifetime, but fought Ahab at the Battle of Qarqar (853 BC). The Kurkh Monolith names "Ahab the Israelite." Assyria would call Israel "Bit Humri" (House of Omri) for a century.

**Mesha, King of Moab** — fl. ~853 BC onward
Paid tribute to Omri and Ahab. Rebelled after Ahab's death. The Mesha Stele (~830 BC) names Omri — one of the earliest extra-biblical attestations of an Israelite king.

## Key Events (~885–835 BC)

**~885 BC — Zimri assassinates Elah, and Omri marches**
Zimri kills Elah in Tirzah and reigns 7 days. The army immediately proclaims Omri king. Omri besieges Tirzah; Zimri burns the palace and dies in it.
1 Kings 16:9–20

**~885–880 BC — Civil War: Omri vs. Tibni**
Five-year standoff between Omri's faction and Tibni son of Ginath. Tibni dies; Omri becomes sole king over Israel.
1 Kings 16:21–22

**~880 BC — Omri buys the hill of Samaria**
Purchased from Shemer for two talents of silver. A politically brilliant move — no tribal loyalties attached to Samaria. Built as a royal city from scratch, rivaling Jerusalem. It would stand 150+ years.
1 Kings 16:24

**~880–874 BC — Omri humbles Moab; treaty with Phoenicia**
Forced tribute from Moab (recorded on the Mesha Stele: "Omri, king of Israel, oppressed Moab many days"). Arranged Ahab's marriage to Jezebel of Sidon — sealing the Phoenician alliance.
Mesha Stele; 1 Kings 16:31

**~874 BC — Omri dies; Ahab succeeds**
Biblical assessment: "did more evil than all before him" (1 Kings 16:25) — a covenantal verdict on his Baal-friendly policies, not a military one. He was Israel's most powerful king.
1 Kings 16:28

**~874–853 BC — Elijah's ministry: drought, Carmel, Naboth**
Ahab's reign brings the full consequence of Omri's Phoenician alliance: Jezebel's Baal worship goes institutional. Elijah declares drought; Mount Carmel showdown; Naboth's vineyard seized (1 Kings 21). The prophetic confrontation with the Omride legacy.
1 Kings 17–21

**~853 BC — Battle of Qarqar: Ahab vs. Shalmaneser III**
Ahab joins the Syrian coalition against Assyria at Qarqar. The Kurkh Monolith records "Ahab the Israelite" with 2,000 chariots — the largest contingent. Ahab dies the same year at Ramoth-Gilead.
Kurkh Monolith; 1 Kings 22:34–37

**~852–841 BC — Joram of Israel rules; Elisha's ministry begins**
The third-generation Omride. Elisha takes over Elijah's mantle. Moab rebels (2 Kings 3 — corroborated by the Mesha Stele). The Omride dynasty begins to unravel.
2 Kings 1–9; 2 Kings 3

**~841 BC — Jehu's Revolution: the Omride dynasty ends**
Jehu (anointed by Elisha's servant) kills Joram of Israel and Ahaziah of Judah in one campaign. Jezebel thrown from the window; Ahab's 70 sons killed. Four generations of Omride power ends in a single afternoon.
2 Kings 9–10

**~841–835 BC — Athaliah seizes Judah; Omride line in Jerusalem**
Omri's granddaughter Athaliah kills the royal family of Judah and rules as queen. The infant Joash is hidden in the temple by Jehosheba. After 6 years, Jehoiada the priest reveals Joash; Athaliah is killed. The Omride line in Judah ends.
2 Kings 11:1–16

## Books in Context

**Written during this era (narrative record):**
1 Kings 16–22 and 2 Kings 1–10 — Omri's rise through Ahab's death (1 Kings 16–22); Ahaziah, Joram, and Jehu's revolution (2 Kings 1–10). The Deuteronomistic History's sharpest negative verdict on any dynasty.

**Set during this era:**
The Elijah–Elisha cycle (1 Kings 17–2 Kings 13) spans the entire Omride era. Includes Naboth's vineyard (1 Kings 21). Also the Mesha Stele (~830 BC) — extra-biblical; Moab's own account of throwing off the Omride yoke.

**Key cross-references:**
- 1 Kings 16:21–28 — Omri's reign summary
- 1 Kings 16:29–34 — Ahab introduced
- Micah 6:16 — "the statutes of Omri" condemned 150 years later
- 2 Kings 8:18, 8:26 — Athaliah's Omride lineage traced
- 2 Kings 11:1 — Athaliah nearly wipes out the Davidic line

## Extra-Biblical Evidence

The Mesha Stele (Moabite Stone, ~830 BC) explicitly names Omri — "Omri, king of Israel, oppressed Moab many days" — making him one of the most archaeologically confirmed Israelite monarchs. Assyrian records used "Bit Humri" (House of Omri) to refer to the northern kingdom of Israel for over a century after the Omride dynasty ended with Jehu's revolution in ~841 BC. The Kurkh Monolith names his son "Ahab the Israelite." Omri was invisible in world memory for centuries — then archaeology confirmed him as one of the most consequential kings of Iron Age Israel.

## Then and Now

**Then**
Omri was politically brilliant but covenantally disastrous. He built Samaria (which stood 150 years), subdued Moab, and allied with Phoenicia — the ancient Near East took notice. But his real legacy was the Baal worship Jezebel brought in, and the Omride DNA he planted in Judah through Athaliah, which nearly wiped out the Davidic line entirely (2 Kings 11:1). The Assyrians remembered his name; the prophets remembered his sin (Micah 6:16).

**Now**
Competence and covenant faithfulness are not the same thing. A leader can be effective, strategic, and historically significant — and still be building on sand. Omri's dynasty lasted 4 kings across 44 years. Jehu's revolution ended it in one afternoon. What outlasted the Omride dynasty was not their military power but the prophetic word that confronted it. The statutes of Omri (Micah 6:16) were still being invoked as a warning 150 years later.
