## The Setup

From Exodus 40: God's glory fills the tabernacle. Israel is camped at Sinai. The question Leviticus answers: *how does a holy God live among an unholy people?*

## The Five Offerings (Chapters 1–7)

**Burnt Offering — עֹלָה / Olah / "that which goes up"**
Entirely consumed. Total dedication — nothing kept by the offerer.

**Grain Offering — מִנְחָה / Minchah / "gift / tribute"**
Flour, oil, incense. Accompanies animal offerings. Acknowledges God as provider.

**Peace Offering — שְׁלָמִים / Shelamim / "wholeness"**
Shared meal: parts for God, parts for priest, parts for worshipper. Communion.

**Sin Offering — חַטָּאת / Chatat / "to miss the mark"**
Addresses unintentional sin or impurity. Restores access to the holy space.

**Guilt Offering — אָשָׁם / Asham / "liability / debt"**
For specific violations. Includes restitution — repair, not just forgiveness.

Each offering addresses a different aspect of the human–God relationship.

---

## The Hinge — Chapter 16: Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur)

**Goat 1 — Sacrificed**
Blood brought into the Most Holy Place. Propitiation — the holiness of God is satisfied.

**Goat 2 — Azazel (Scapegoat)**
Sins confessed over it, driven into the wilderness. Expiation — guilt is removed and sent away.

Two goats, two problems: the presence of holiness and the reality of sin. Once a year. High priest only.

## Hardest Moment — Chapter 10: Nadab and Abihu

Aaron's sons offer "unauthorized fire" and are immediately killed. Not arbitrary cruelty — the point is that proximity to holiness is not casual. The extensive ordination ritual that precedes this existed precisely because the stakes were this high.

## Key Words

**קָדוֹשׁ / Qadosh** *(Holy)*
Appears 152× in Leviticus. "Set apart / other / categorically different." Ontological, not just moral — God is holy because he is unlike everything else.

**כָּפַר / Kaphar** *(Atonement)*
"To cover / ransom / wipe clean." Root of Yom Kippur. Closer to satisfying a debt than simply forgiving it.

**טָמֵא / Tame** *(Unclean)*
Ritual impurity — a state, not a sin. Being tamei is not morally culpable; it means you cannot approach the holy space until resolved.

## Then → Now

Israel asked: how do we live next to a presence this holy? We've largely stopped asking the question. Leviticus is jarring because it assumes a sense of divine holiness most modern people — including regular churchgoers — no longer have.

---

## Verdict

> Holiness has a cost. Mediation is necessary. You cannot simply walk into the presence of the holy — something has to be done first.
