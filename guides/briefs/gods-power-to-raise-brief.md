# God's Power to Raise — Amos 9:11 & Samson

> Teaching · Restoration, Ruin, and the God Who Rebuilds · Amos 9:11 · Judges 13–16

Placement: This teaching holds Amos 9:11 and the Samson narrative (Judges 13–16) together as complementary texts on God's power to raise the fallen. The national promise (the restored booth of David) and the personal story (the blind prisoner at the millstone) speak with one voice: the ruins are not the verdict.

---

## The Big Idea

God does not abandon what He built just because it has collapsed. The fallen booth, the blind prisoner, the ruined life — these are not the end of the story. They are the setting for His most dramatic act of raising power.

---

## Anchor Texts

> "In that day I will restore David's fallen shelter — I will repair its broken walls and restore its ruins — and will rebuild it as it used to be." — Amos 9:11 (NIV)

> "Sovereign LORD, remember me. Please, God, strengthen me just once more." — Judges 16:28 (NIV)

---

## Word Studies — Amos 9:11

### סֻכַּת — sukkāt — "booth / tent / shelter"
**Not the Temple — a field booth**
God deliberately chooses the humblest word. The Davidic dynasty is not called a palace — it is a leaky field shelter. He names the collapse for what it is before raising it.

### הַנֹּפֶלֶת — hannōpelet — "the fallen one"
**Active participle — currently fallen**
Not "was fallen." Present tense. God speaks the raising promise while the booth is still down. He does not wait for signs of life before announcing restoration.

### אָקִים — āqîm — "I will raise / lift up"
**God is the subject — not the booth**
The fallen booth does not raise itself. The verb's subject is entirely God. This is the grammar of grace — the initiative belongs to the one who raises, not the one who fell.

## Samson's Arc — Judges 13–16

**Ch. 13 — Set Apart**
Miraculous birth. Nazirite vow from the womb. The Spirit comes upon him (13:25). Built for purpose before he takes his first breath.

**Chs. 14–15 — Progressive Compromise**
Philistine wife. The riddle. Foxes and torches. Each entanglement deeper than the last. The Spirit still comes — but the choices grow darker.

**16:1–16 — The Three Lies**
Delilah asks the secret three times. Three lies — each one bringing the enemy closer. Then: "with such nagging she prodded him day after day until he was sick to death of it." Weariness is how the enemy gets the secret.

**16:17–21 — The Fall**
He tells her everything. The shaving. He wakes and shakes himself as before.

> "He did not know that the LORD had left him." — Judges 16:20
*The most tragic verse in Judges.*

**16:22 — The Hair Begins to Grow**
While he is still in prison. Before the prayer. Before any act of faith. The first movement of restoration is God's.

> "But the hair of his head began to grow again after it had been shaved." — Judges 16:22
*The smallest verse with the biggest weight.*

**16:28–30 — The Prayer and the Raising**
"Sovereign LORD, remember me. Please, God, strengthen me just once more." No bargaining. No promises. Just: *remember me.* God answers. The pillars fall. More in death than in life.

---

## Three Sermon Movements

**Movement 1 — God Names the Ruins Honestly**
*(Amos 9:11a · Judges 16:20–21)*
He doesn't call the booth a palace. He doesn't pretend Samson is still standing. The God who raises is also the God who sees clearly. He raises *from* the ruins — not around them. The honesty of "the fallen booth" and "he did not know the LORD had left him" is not condemnation — it is the prerequisite for genuine restoration.

*Illustration: A surgeon who lies about the diagnosis cannot heal. God's willingness to name the collapse exactly is what makes his promise to raise it credible.*

**Movement 2 — The Hair Begins to Grow**
*(Judges 16:22 · Amos 9:11b)*
Before the prayer. Before the act of faith. While Samson is still chained to the millstone. The first movement of restoration is God's — not ours. The booth does not prop itself up. The hair grows in the dark, unnoticed, unannounced. Something is being rebuilt before you can see it.

*Illustration: Grace often precedes awareness. The restoration has begun before the prayer is formed. The "day" of Amos 9:11 is God's initiative, not Israel's recovery plan.*

**Movement 3 — Strength for the Moment That Matters**
*(Judges 16:28–30 · Amos 9:12 + Acts 15:16–17)*
Samson doesn't get his eyes back. He doesn't walk out of prison as before. He gets strength *for the moment that counts*. The restored booth in Amos isn't Israel returning to Solomon's golden age — it's a remnant inheriting a bigger purpose. God raises things *forward*, not just back.

*Illustration: James quotes Amos 9:11 at the Jerusalem Council to justify including Gentiles (Acts 15:16–17). The raised booth became the church — more than anyone who heard Amos originally imagined.*

---

## The Two Verses

**The Most Dangerous Verse — Judges 16:20**
"He did not know that the LORD had left him."
Sin's most dangerous effect is not guilt — it is anaesthesia. You stop feeling the absence. Samson shook himself "as at other times" — his muscle memory was intact, but the power was gone. The terrifying thing is not that God left. It is that Samson did not notice.

**The Turning Verse — Judges 16:22**
"But the hair of his head began to grow again after it had been shaved."
The shortest verse in the Samson narrative — and the most important. Placed before the prayer, before repentance, before anything Samson does. God moves first. The "but" (וַיָּחֶל) is the hinge of the whole story.

## The Two Texts Side by Side

|  | Amos 9:11 | Samson |
|---|---|---|
| What fell | David's dynasty — a nation | A consecrated man |
| Cause of fall | Covenant failure, injustice | Compromise, entanglement |
| Condition when God acts | Currently fallen (active participle) | Blind, chained, mocked |
| Who moves first | God — *āqîm* (I will raise) | God — hair grows before prayer |
| The outcome | Greater than before (Acts 15) | More in death than in life |

## Pressure Points for the Teacher

**Was Samson's death suicide or sacrifice?**
Judges gives no verdict — the text is intentionally ambiguous. The author focuses on the prayer and the result, not the ethics of the method. Hebrews 11:32 lists Samson among the heroes of faith without resolving this.

**Did God restore Samson because of the prayer, or had it already begun (v.22)?**
Both. The hair growing (v.22) shows God's prior grace; the prayer (v.28) shows Samson's response to what he sensed. Grace precedes, response follows — neither cancels the other.

**Amos 9:11 speaks to a nation — how does it apply to individuals?**
The principle is the character of God, not just the specific promise. A God who rebuilds fallen dynasties is the same God who rebuilds fallen people. Samson's story is the personal illustration of the national promise.

**"Once more" — is this repentance or just a request for power?**
The prayer is minimal — "remember me" (covenant language) and "strengthen me." There is no elaborate confession. God answers anyway. This is not an endorsement of prayerlessness — it is evidence that even a cry from the ruins is enough.

## Cross-References

**Acts 15:16–17**
James quotes Amos 9:11 at the Jerusalem Council to justify Gentile inclusion. The raised booth of David = the church. God raised it bigger than it ever was.

**Hebrews 11:32**
Samson listed among the heroes of faith — alongside Gideon, Barak, David. The NT does not overlook the failure; it includes him anyway. That is the point.

**Ezekiel 37:1–14**
Valley of dry bones — the same raising power applied to a whole nation. "Can these bones live?" is the same question as "can this booth be raised?" Same answer.

**Romans 8:11**
"He who raised Christ from the dead will also give life to your mortal bodies." The raising power is not retired. It is resident.

**Judges 16:30b**
"He killed many more when he died than while he lived." The greatest act of raising power came through the ruins — not despite them.

---

## Then → Now

**Then**
Amos preached to a prosperous, self-satisfied nation that could not imagine collapse. Samson had every gift and squandered them. Both are pictures of covenant people who experienced God's absence after sustained unfaithfulness.

**Now**
The sermon addresses anyone who has experienced gifting followed by failure — and is wondering if what collapsed can be raised. The answer from both texts: God raises things *forward*, not just back. The raised thing is often greater than the original.

---

## Verdict

The God of Amos 9:11 is a God who walks into a field of rubble and says "I will rebuild this." The God of Judges 16 is a God who watches a man grind grain in the dark and starts growing his hair back before the man has even prayed. The ruins are not the verdict. They are the address where God shows up.
